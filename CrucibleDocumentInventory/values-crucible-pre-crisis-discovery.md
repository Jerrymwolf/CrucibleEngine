# Values Crucible: Pre-Crisis Values Discovery

## Design Philosophy

**The gap is the lesson.** Without knowing what people believe before pressure hits, there's nothing meaningful to compare against. This phase creates the baseline—carefully, positively, and with psychological safety.

### Guiding Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Private → Shared → Public** | Individual reflection first, then dyads, then team. Never expose before ready. |
| **Reflective → Interactive → Committed** | Think alone, discuss together, commit publicly. Deepen gradually. |
| **Positive framing** | Values are strengths to build on, not deficits to fix. Leave people feeling empowered. |
| **Storytelling reveals truth** | Self-report alone is unreliable. Stories about lived experience surface actual values. |
| **Commitment creates accountability** | Written commitments become the mirror held up in debrief. |

### Adult Learning Foundation

From Mezirow's transformative learning theory: Adults learn best when they can critically reflect on their existing beliefs in a supportive environment, then test those beliefs through dialogue with others.

From Knowles' andragogy: Adults need to understand WHY they're learning something, connect it to their experience, and see immediate relevance.

From your BEI methodology: "The most important thing in the interview is your interviewee: how they feel about themselves during and after. You want each person to leave feeling better about themselves than they did when they started."

---

## Phase Structure Overview

| Phase | Duration | Focus | Output Captured |
|-------|----------|-------|-----------------|
| **1. Personal Values Discovery** | 45 min | Individual reflection | Private values profile |
| **2. Team Values Alignment** | 60 min | Collaborative convergence | Team values + commitment |
| **3. Organizational Values Integration** | 30 min | (OPTIONAL) Client values | Org values as test layer |
| **4. Scenario Briefing** | 15 min | Stakes and context | Baseline complete |

**Total: 2 hours (or 2.5 with organizational module)**

# PHASE 1: Personal Values Discovery (45 min)

## Purpose

Surface each participant's authentic personal values through a combination of intuitive selection, reflective writing, and storytelling. Create psychological safety by keeping everything private until participants choose to share.

**The deeper purpose:** Leave every participant feeling clearer about who they are, proud of values they've lived, and connected to their own strength. This isn't assessment—it's affirmation with awareness.

## Facilitator Stance (From Your BEI Training)

> "You are not a cold, 'objective' researcher. You are a coach. You are a trusted colleague. Your goals (collecting 'data', taking good notes) are secondary. The most important thing is your participant: how they feel about themselves during and after. You want each and every person to leave feeling better about themselves than they did when they started."

This stance applies to the entire pre-crisis phase. Every activity should leave people feeling seen, valued, and empowered.

## Learning Outcomes

- Clarity on personal core values (not aspirational, but actual)
- Pride in moments when values have guided action
- Foundation for comparing with team and (optionally) organizational values
- Emotional readiness to engage with team process

## Activity 1.1: Values Quick Sort (10 min)

**Adapted from your Coast Guard Commander Workshop**

### Setup
Participants receive a digital deck of 40 value cards (or physical cards if in-person). Each card has:
- Value name (e.g., "Integrity")
- Brief definition (e.g., "Acting in accordance with my beliefs, even when difficult")

### Process

**Round 1 — Intuitive Sort (3 min)**
> "Sort these values into three piles as quickly as possible. Don't overthink it—go with your gut:
> - **Essential to who I am** (limit: 10 max)
> - **Important but not core**
> - **Not particularly important to me**
> 
> You have 3 minutes. Speed matters—your first instinct is usually truest."

**Round 2 — Narrow to Five (4 min)**
> "Now look at your 'Essential' pile. Narrow it down to exactly five values. These are your core values—the ones you'd be unwilling to compromise even under pressure.
>
> If you have fewer than five in your Essential pile, that's fine. If you have more than ten, you need to make hard choices. That difficulty is part of the learning."

**Round 3 — Rank Order (3 min)**
> "Finally, rank your five core values from 1 (most important) to 5. When two values conflict, which wins? That determines the order."

### Facilitator Notes
- Emphasize speed in Round 1 to bypass social desirability bias
- Remind participants this is private—no one will see unless they choose to share
- The struggle in Round 2 is valuable—don't rescue them from it

### System Capture
```
Individual Values Profile:
- Top 5 values (ranked)
- Timestamp
- Participant ID (anonymized for research)
```

## Activity 1.2: Values Meaning Exercise (15 min)

**Adapted from your Senior Leadership Conference design**

### Purpose
Values words mean different things to different people. "Integrity" to one person might mean "honesty"; to another, "consistency"; to a third, "courage to dissent." This exercise surfaces what each value actually means to the participant.

### Process

**Individual Reflection (10 min)**

Participants write brief responses for their top 3 values:

> "For each of your top three values, answer these questions in a few sentences:
>
> 1. **What does this value mean to you?** (Not the dictionary definition—YOUR definition)
>
> 2. **What does this value look like when you're living it?** (Specific behaviors others would see)
>
> 3. **What does it feel like when this value is honored?** (Internal experience)
>
> 4. **What does it feel like when this value is violated?** (Internal experience)"

**Example Response:**

| Value | Integrity |
|-------|-----------|
| **My definition** | Doing what I say I'll do, especially when no one is watching |
| **Behaviors when living it** | Following through on commitments even when inconvenient; admitting mistakes; saying "no" rather than over-promising |
| **Feels like when honored** | Grounded, clear-headed, proud |
| **Feels like when violated** | Anxious, ashamed, disconnected from myself |

**Brief Pair Share (5 min)**
> "Turn to one person near you. Share ONE of your values and what it means to you. The other person's job is simply to listen and reflect back what they heard. Then switch. Two minutes each."

### Facilitator Notes
- Pair share builds safety incrementally before team work
- Reflecting back (not advising or comparing) builds trust
- This exercise often surfaces that people use the same word but mean different things

### System Capture
```
Values Definitions:
- Value 1: word + participant's definition + behaviors
- Value 2: word + participant's definition + behaviors  
- Value 3: word + participant's definition + behaviors
```

## Activity 1.3: Values Story — The Heart of Discovery (20 min)

**Adapted from your BEI Protocol**

### Purpose

Stories reveal values-in-action more truthfully than self-report. But equally important: recalling a moment of values-driven action reconnects people to their own strength. This isn't data collection—it's helping people remember who they are at their best.

### Facilitator Framing

> "We've identified values and defined them. Now I want you to remember a time you actually lived them.
>
> Think of a specific moment in your professional life—a challenging situation where you felt compelled to act based on your values. This doesn't have to be dramatic. It could be a small moment: speaking up when it would have been easier to stay silent, making a harder choice when an easier one was available, standing by someone when others didn't.
>
> What I'm looking for is a moment you're proud of. A time when, looking back, you think: 'Yes. That was me at my best.'
>
> Take a moment to let a story surface. We have time. I'd rather you find a story that really matters to you."

### Individual Reflection (10 min)

Participants write about their moment:

> "Once you have a story in mind, write briefly:
>
> 1. **Set the scene.** What was happening? What made it challenging?
>
> 2. **What was the pressure or temptation?** What would have been the easier path?
>
> 3. **What did you actually do?** Be specific.
>
> 4. **What value were you honoring?** (It might be one you selected, or one you didn't name until now.)
>
> 5. **How did it feel?** In the moment, and afterward.
>
> This is for you. You'll have the option to share, but only what you choose."

### Triad Share — Witnessing Each Other (10 min)

> "Form groups of three. Each person will have about 2.5 minutes to share their story—or as much of it as they're comfortable sharing.
>
> **The listeners' job is simple but important:**
> - Be fully present. This person is sharing something real.
> - When they finish, reflect back the value(s) you heard in their story.
> - That's it. No advice. No comparison to your own story. Just witness and name what you heard.
>
> Often others see our values more clearly than we do ourselves. And being seen—really seen—is a gift."

### Facilitator Notes

- This activity often surfaces emotion. That's appropriate—values connected to action are meaningful.
- Some participants will share stories that reveal different values than they self-selected. Don't correct; notice. This is valuable data and valuable awareness.
- The triad format provides support without full-group exposure.
- Naming values they heard (not evaluating or advising) keeps it affirming.
- If time is short, dyads work. But triads provide more diverse witnessing.

### The Emotional Purpose

From your BEI training: *"When people are supported and validated in their time with you, they become more committed... and to their own vision of themselves at their best."*

This activity isn't just baseline data. It's reconnection to strength. Participants enter the simulation having just remembered: "I have faced hard moments before. I have acted from my values before. I can do it again."

### System Capture
```
Values Story:
- Story text (private unless shared)
- Self-identified values demonstrated
- Peer-identified values (from triad)
- Alignment/divergence flag (if different values named)
```

---

## Phase 1 Closing

> "You now have:
> - Your five core values, ranked
> - Your personal definitions of what those values mean
> - A story that anchors those values in your lived experience
>
> This is your values baseline. In the simulation ahead, you'll face moments that test these values. Some tests will be obvious. Others will be subtle—you won't realize your values are at stake until afterward.
>
> The goal isn't perfection. The goal is awareness. And you've already built the foundation for that awareness."

# PHASE 2: Team Values Alignment (60 min)

## Purpose

Move from individual values to shared team values through a collaborative, generative process. Create team commitment that can be referenced during the simulation and compared against in debrief.

**The deeper purpose:** Build genuine connection through values. When people discover shared ground—and honor differences—something shifts. Teams become more than functional units; they become people who've seen each other's core.

## Design Principle: Convergence Without Coercion

Teams should discover overlap, not force agreement. Where values differ, the goal is understanding, not homogenization. The team's final values statement reflects genuine shared ground, not lowest-common-denominator compromise.

**This should feel like discovery, not negotiation.**

---

## Activity 2.1: Values Gallery — Seeing Each Other (15 min)

### Setup
Each team member posts their top 5 values (just the words, not definitions) on a shared digital board or physical wall. No names attached initially.

### Process

**Silent Gallery Walk (5 min)**
> "Walk through the gallery of your team's values. Don't talk yet—just look.
>
> Notice:
> - Which values appear multiple times? (There's usually more overlap than people expect.)
> - Which values are unique to one person?
> - What surprises you?
> - What themes emerge?
>
> Let yourself be curious. Every value here matters to someone on your team."

**Claim Your Values (3 min)**
> "Now, step forward and put your name next to your values. Own them.
>
> Look around. You're seeing each other more fully now."

**Facilitated Noticing (7 min)**
> "Let's name what we see together:
> - Where is there common ground?
> - Where is there beautiful difference?
> - What does this tell us about who we are as a team?"

**Visual Output:**

```
TEAM HAVEN — VALUES GALLERY

████████████ INTEGRITY (4 members: Sarah, Marcus, Jin, Priya)
████████     ACCOUNTABILITY (3 members: Marcus, Jin, Amir)  
██████       COURAGE (2 members: Sarah, Priya)
██████       FAIRNESS (2 members: Amir, Jin)
████         EXCELLENCE (2 members: Sarah, Marcus)
██           LOYALTY (1 member: Amir)
██           INNOVATION (1 member: Priya)
██           COMPASSION (1 member: Jin)
```

### Facilitator Notes
- The reveal of names often creates connection: "I didn't know you cared about that too."
- Unique values are treasures, not problems. Name them: "Jin, you're the one holding Compassion for this team. That matters."
- The visual makes convergence visible without forcing it

## Activity 2.2: Values Definitions Dialogue (20 min)

### Purpose
Same word often means different things. "Accountability" to one person might mean "taking responsibility"; to another, "holding others responsible." Teams need shared understanding, not just shared words.

### Process

**Select Top Values (5 min)**
> "Based on the gallery, your team will now select 5 values that will guide you through the simulation. These should be values that:
> - Multiple team members share, OR
> - The team agrees are essential even if only one person named them
>
> You have 5 minutes to select your 5 team values."

**Define Together (15 min)**
> "For each value, discuss:
> - What does this value mean to us as a team?
> - What behaviors would we see if we're living this value?
> - What behaviors would indicate we're NOT living this value?
>
> Write a single sentence definition for each value that everyone can agree to."

**Example Team Definitions:**

| Value | Team Definition | Living It | Violating It |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Integrity** | We do what we say, especially under pressure | Following through on commitments; admitting mistakes quickly | Making promises we can't keep; hiding problems |
| **Accountability** | We own our decisions and their consequences | Taking responsibility without blaming; learning from failures | Finger-pointing; "that's not my job" |
| **Courage** | We speak up when it matters, even when it's hard | Raising concerns; challenging bad decisions | Staying silent to avoid conflict |

### System Capture
```
Team Values:
- 5 values (ranked or unranked)
- Team definition for each
- Positive indicators (behaviors)
- Negative indicators (violations)
```

## Activity 2.3: Team Commitment — A Promise to Ourselves (15 min)

**Adapted from your Command Values Statement exercise**

### Purpose

A written commitment creates accountability. But more than that: saying out loud what we intend to do, together, creates something real. Words spoken in commitment have weight.

When teams return to this statement in debrief, the gap between intention and action becomes visible and discussable. But right now, this is about hope. About declaring who we want to be.

### Process

**Draft Commitment (8 min)**

> "As a team, craft a commitment statement. Start with:
>
> **'When pressure mounts and we face difficult decisions, we commit to...'**
>
> Your statement should:
> - Reflect your team values
> - Be specific enough to be testable (you could fail at it)
> - Be something every person genuinely agrees to
>
> This isn't corporate language. This is what you actually promise each other. Write it the way you'd say it."

**Example Commitment Statements:**

> *"When pressure mounts, we commit to pausing before reacting—actually stopping to ask which value is at stake. We commit to protecting people first, even when the numbers suffer. We commit to speaking up when we see drift, even if we're the only voice. We will own our decisions together, win or lose."*

> *"When pressure mounts, we commit to honesty over comfort—telling each other the truth even when it's hard to hear. We commit to making decisions we can explain to the people affected. We will not sacrifice our integrity for speed."*

**The Affirmation (5 min)**

> "Read your statement aloud as a team. Have one person read it, or read it together.
>
> Does every word feel true? Is there anything that should change?
>
> When you're ready, each team member signs. This isn't a legal contract—it's a promise to yourselves and each other about who you want to be when it gets hard."

**Facilitator Note:** Make this moment matter. A brief pause, acknowledgment: "You've just made a commitment. That means something."

**The Mirror That's Coming (Don't Say This Yet)**

This commitment will be displayed during debrief, alongside the team's actual decisions. The gap between intention and action is where learning lives. But for now, let teams sit in the hope of their commitment.

### System Capture
```
Team Commitment:
- Full statement text
- Timestamp
- Digital signatures (all team members)
- Recording flag (if read aloud)
```

## Activity 2.4: Decision Process Agreement (10 min)

### Purpose
When crisis hits, teams often default to whoever speaks loudest or whoever has highest rank. Agreeing on decision process beforehand creates structure for values-aligned decision-making.

### Process

> "In the simulation, your team will face decisions with time pressure. How will you decide?
>
> Discuss and select your team's decision process:"

**Options:**

| Process | Description | Best When |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **Consensus** | Everyone must agree before action | Stakes are high and time permits |
| **Consultative** | Leader decides after hearing all voices | Time is limited but input matters |
| **Majority Vote** | 50%+ agreement carries the decision | Speed needed, stakes moderate |
| **Designated Leader** | One person decides, others support | Emergency situations |
| **Values Check** | Before any decision, name the value at stake | Always |

> "Most teams use different processes for different situations. Agree on your defaults and when you'd shift."

### System Capture
```
Decision Process:
- Default process selected
- Conditions for shifting to other processes
- Designated leader (if applicable)
```

---

## Phase 2 Closing

> "Your team now has:
> - Five shared values with agreed definitions
> - A commitment statement you've all signed
> - A decision process for when pressure hits
>
> These are your stated values. In the simulation ahead, you'll discover your enacted values—the values that actually show up when the stakes are real.
>
> The goal isn't perfection. It's awareness. And when there's a gap, that gap is where the learning lives."

# PHASE 3: Organizational Values Integration (OPTIONAL — 30 min)

## When to Use

Use this phase when:
- The client organization has defined values (e.g., Apple, Google, a specific company)
- The learning objective includes testing organizational values, not just personal/team values
- There's interest in exploring alignment (or tension) between personal and organizational values

Skip this phase when:
- Participants are from multiple organizations
- The focus is purely on personal leadership development
- Time is constrained

---

## Setup: The Organization's Values

Before the session, the facilitator inputs the client organization's values into the system.

**Example: Apple**
```
APPLE'S STATED VALUES:
1. Accessibility
2. Education  
3. Environment
4. Inclusion and Diversity
5. Privacy
6. Supplier Responsibility
```

**Example: Coast Guard**
```
COAST GUARD CORE VALUES:
1. Honor
2. Respect
3. Devotion to Duty
```

## Activity 3.1: Organizational Values Mapping (15 min)

### Process

**Individual Reflection (5 min)**
> "Look at your organization's stated values. For each one, rate:
> - How well do I understand what this value means in practice? (1-5)
> - How consistently do I see this value demonstrated in my organization? (1-5)
> - How aligned is this value with my personal values? (1-5)"

**Team Discussion (10 min)**
> "Share your ratings with your team. Discuss:
> - Where is there alignment between organizational values and your team values?
> - Where is there tension or gap?
> - Which organizational value is most at risk of being compromised under pressure?"

### System Capture
```
Organizational Values Alignment:
- Per-person ratings (understanding, demonstration, alignment)
- Team-identified tensions
- Team-identified risks
```

# PHASE 4: Scenario Briefing (15 min)

## Purpose

Transition from values discovery into the simulation world. Create emotional stakes without overwhelming. Establish the Concordia/Meridian context.

## Process

**The Stakes (5 min)**

> Video or facilitator narrative introducing:
> - Meridian BioSciences and Celarithin
> - The 340,000 cancer patients who depend on production
> - Concordia Island's interconnected parishes
> - Your team's role in the supply chain

**The Emotional Anchor (3 min)**

> A letter from a patient's family:
>
> *"Dear Meridian Team,*
>
> *My daughter got eighteen more months because of you. Eighteen months to see her son start kindergarten. To finish the book she was writing. To tell everyone she loved exactly what they meant to her.*
>
> *I know you don't see us. We're just numbers on a shipping manifest. But we see you. Every dose that arrives is someone's extra day, extra week, extra month.*
>
> *Thank you for what you do.*
>
> *— Jennifer M., Ohio"*

This letter appears in the break room at every Meridian facility. Every worker knows why they're there.

**And yet.**

What happens when protecting those 340,000 patients requires asking your people to work through a hurricane? When production pressure and worker safety collide?

**Your Parish Assignment (5 min)**

> "Your team will manage [PARISH NAME]. Here's what you need to know..."
>
> [Parish-specific briefing card with role, assets, vulnerabilities, dependencies]

**Rules of Engagement (2 min)**

> "In the simulation:
> - Decisions are made as a team using the process you agreed on
> - Every decision will be captured with your stated rationale
> - The Crucible Master controls intensity and can intervene
> - Your values commitment will not be visible during the crisis—but it will be visible in debrief
>
> Any questions before we begin?"

## Research Applications

This phase generates rich data for your ValuesPrism research:

1. **Stated values** → Compare to decisions made under pressure
2. **Values definitions** → Analyze how same word means different things
3. **Values stories** → ABCD coding opportunities (affect, behavior, cognition, desire)
