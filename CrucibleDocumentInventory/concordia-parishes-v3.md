# Concordia, canon v3

One island, one company, one cure, one storm. Six parishes, each with something the others need. The question the island asks every leader during Hurricane Midas is the same: **what will you spend to keep the medicine moving, and whose people will you spend?**

This document is the world bible under the canon Jerry decided on 2026-10-21 ([[decision-2026-10-21-parish-leadership-redesign]]). Where it disagrees with [[concordia-all-parishes]] or [[concordia-keystone-parish]], this document wins. Numbers are design numbers: good enough to argue over at a table, not engineering.

## The story in one page

Concordia is a volcanic island, fourteen kilometres across, with a crater lake at its heart and a ring of farmed terraces falling to the sea. Seventy years ago a dam was built across the crater's northern notch; the lake became the island's water and its power, and the river that spills north through the gorge became the spine of its capital.

Forty years ago the kelp beds off the western shore turned out to contain thalassarin, a compound that stops a family of blood and bone cancers from growing. The drug made from it, **Celarithin**, is given every two weeks to **340,000 patients** on four continents. It cannot be frozen and it keeps for twenty-one days. Every dose on earth is made here, and nowhere else.

**Meridian BioSciences** owns the cure and most of the island's working life. It does not own the island. Concordia has a government, a hospital, a police force and a mayor, and they all sit in the northern city at Summit. Meridian's headquarters sits across the river from them at Sterling. The two have shared a wall, a bridge and a reservoir for forty years, and they have never once agreed on who comes first when there is not enough.

The supply chain runs clockwise around the ring road: **Gateway** cuts the kelp and flies the world in and out; **Heartland** holds the water, the power and the works that turn kelp into thalassarin concentrate; **Keystone** turns concentrate into Celarithin in twelve clean rooms; **Haven** puts it on a ship. Every link is the only one of its kind. Stop any parish for four days and patients abroad miss an infusion.

Hurricane Midas, Category 4, arrives in the middle of a consignment. Over four quarters (preparation, approach, landfall, recovery) each parish's leadership will decide, again and again, between the doses the world is waiting for and the people in front of them. The board keeps two counts and never lets either be forgotten: **doses shipped** and **people safe**.

## Geography that matters

The island art (`crucible/board/concordia-1400.jpg`) is canon. North is up.

| Where | What | Parish |
|---|---|---|
| Centre | Crater lake, Concordia Dam on the northern rim, the lakeshore village of Caldera, the kelp works on the river just below the dam | Heartland |
| North, west bank of the river | The capital: civic grid, Hospital Hill, the old riverside quarter ("Riverside") | Summit |
| North, east bank and headland | Meridian's campus towers, executive housing on the headland, the uplink tower | Sterling |
| East coast | Deep-water port, container and reefer yards, fuel terminal, union hall | Haven |
| South | Industrial district: the Celarithin plant, QA, cold vault, and the Southfield housing flat along the shore | Keystone |
| West | Airport, the Strand resorts, the Mole (fleet harbour) on the north-west headland | Gateway |

**Water runs north.** Anything the dam releases goes through the gorge and into the river that divides Summit from Sterling. Riverside (Summit) and the lower campus (Sterling) are the spillway's floodplain. Heartland's hardest decision is therefore a decision about the capital and the company, and both know it.

**Routes.** The ring road (42 km) links the five coastal parishes: Gateway–Summit (north-west), Summit–Sterling (the river bridges), Sterling–Haven (north-east), Haven–Keystone (south-east), Keystone–Gateway (south-west). Two crater roads climb to Heartland: the Gorge Road from Summit beside the river, and the South Switchbacks from Keystone. The Switchbacks slide first; the Gorge Road floods when the spillway opens. Air: one runway at Gateway (closes at 45 mph sustained); two helicopters at Sterling (fly below 40 mph and in the eye). Sea: Haven's harbour closes to departures at T-30h; the tugs can work inside the breakwater until the surge tops it. When the storm breaks a route, aid sent along it does not arrive ([[crucible-calls-for-aid-v3]]).

**Population 24,100.** Summit 6,200 · Haven 4,600 · Heartland 4,200 · Gateway 3,800 · Keystone 3,200 · Sterling 2,100.

## The cure, in numbers the board uses

- A **dose** is one patient's two-week supply. The world needs 170,000 doses a week.
- Keystone makes **24,000 doses a day** on three shifts. The cold vault holds five days of output (120,000 doses), because the drug is filled to order and must sail within days.
- Haven ships a refrigerated consignment every three to four days. The one at the berth when Midas is named is the **Singapore consignment**: 120,000 doses for the Asia-Pacific distributor, due to sail by T-30h. Her ship is the MV *Kestrel Reach*.
- The distributor's contract carries a **reliability clause**: two consecutive late consignments and it may second-source from a biosimilar approved last year in Asia-Pacific. Lower efficacy, but on the shelf. That is the sentence that makes Chicago sweat, and it is why "the island's contract and jobs" sit on the board beside "patients."
- 340,000 patients. Fourteen percent are children. The letter from Jennifer M., Ohio, hangs in every Meridian break room ([[values-crucible-pre-crisis-discovery]]); it stays in the brief.

## Who sits at the tables

Each table is the **leadership council of its parish**: the people who sign the orders. The facilitator (the DM) voices the **persona leaders**: the operational chief who reports to the council and presses it, and the island's principals who press from outside. Six personas carry over from the old corpus with their voices intact (see [[concordia-all-parishes]] for the full fences); one is added.

| Parish | The table is | DM persona | Role of the persona |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gateway | Gateway Parish Council and the harbour board | **Captain Rosa Mendez**, fleet captain | Reports to the council; speaks for 38 crew and every boat |
| Heartland | Board of the Concordia Water & Power Authority, sitting jointly with the works management | **Chief Engineer Yuki Tanaka**, Concordia Dam | Reports to the board; owns the gauges, not the choice |
| Keystone | Meridian Keystone site leadership | **Frank Reeves**, plant manager | Reports to the site leadership; knows every name on every shift |
| Haven | Haven Port Authority board | **Maria Santos**, union representative, Longshore Local 847 | Does not report to anyone at the table; can stop the port |
| Summit | Island Emergency Council, chaired by the Mayor in the Governor's absence | **Mayor Diana Chen** (principal) and **Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo**, Chief Medical Officer, Concordia General | Chen presses for the city; Okonkwo for the hospital |
| Sterling | Meridian Concordia crisis room, the regional leadership team | **Marcus Webb**, Regional President (principal), and **Chicago** (global HQ, by email) | Webb delegates and demands options; Chicago demands doses |

Two personas are **principals** rather than reports (Chen, Webb) because a government and a company are led from the top, and a council of deputies under a principal who says "bring me options" is how both places actually run in a crisis. The Coast Guard cutter *Resolute* (Lt. Cdr. Sarah Park, eight hours out) is a voice the DM may use in landfall and recovery.

Persona name note: the design corpus names the Heartland engineer **Yuki** Tanaka; `crucible/content/personas.json` has Kenji. Canon is Yuki. Fix the content when it is converted.

# GATEWAY

**West. Population 3,800. Meridian employment 60%.** The front door. The kelp fleet, the only runway, the resorts, and the people who see the weather first and are the last anyone thinks of afterwards.

**Produces.** Raw kelp: eighteen boats, twelve active, eight-hour runs to the western beds, 60 tons a day trucked to Heartland's works within four hours of landing. Tourist money: 1,200 rooms on the Strand, 2,000 visitors on an ordinary week. Access: the airport is the island's only fixed-wing link, for people, parts, insulin and the press.

**Needs.** Power and water from Heartland. Diesel for the boats and the airport generators from Haven's terminal. Any real medical care from Summit (the airport clinic stabilises; it does not treat). A clear ring road west to get its own people to the city's shelters.

**Uniquely holds.** The **runway** (the only evacuation and medevac for anyone who cannot sail). The **fleet**: twelve boats that are the island's only shallow-water rescue craft. The **Mole**, a breakwater harbour that shelters boats but funnels surge. 80,000 gallons of aviation fuel and marine diesel. The Strand hotels: concrete, hurricane-rated, 2,000 shelter places that belong to a resort group, not the parish.

**Shares (overlapping).** Evacuation seats, the boats, hotel beds, fifteen small hotel generators, the airport terminal (600 places). Every one of these is also wanted by Summit (medevac, shelter), Keystone (boats for Southfield), Sterling (seats for staff), and Gateway's own residents.

**Tempted to hoard.** Seats for residents over tourists and over other parishes' sick. Boats kept safe in the Mole rather than lent into the storm. The hotels' beds and generators, which the resort group would rather keep for guests.

**Vulnerabilities.** The Mole faces the open ocean: boats must be in 24 hours before landfall, and inside they still take damage. The runway closes at 45 mph; after that the island's only lift is Sterling's two helicopters. Thirty percent of Gateway on any day does not live here and does not know where the shelters are. The kelp pipeline starts here: no harvest for three days and Heartland's works go idle a day later.

**The people.** Captains and crews who were fishermen before they were harvesters; ground crew, controllers and security at the airport; hotel staff; the retirees who came for the view. They believe "the ocean doesn't negotiate" and that visitors do not understand the place. They fear being caught on the water, the runway closing before their families are out, and being forgotten in recovery because they are far from the city.

**Persona: Captain Rosa Mendez.** Few words, each one counts. Knows what the weather will do and which boats are ready. Her husband Daniel manages a Strand hotel and is on no priority list. *"The ocean doesn't give second chances."* *"You can fire me tomorrow. My crew will be alive to hear about it."*

**Core question.** When do you stop betting that you have more time, and whose seat do you give away when you lose the bet?

**What the board reports.** Doses: the harvest that did or did not come in (Heartland's works stock). People: boats at sea, the terminal crowd, the Strand, the *Pacific Harvester*.

# HEARTLAND

**Centre. Population 4,200. Meridian employment 45%.** The crater. The dam, the lake, the water, the works. The people everyone depends on and nobody thinks about until the lights go.

**Produces.** Power: Concordia Dam, 45 MW, eighty percent of the island's electricity. Water: the reservoir and the treatment plant, eight million gallons a day, including the ultrapure line Keystone cannot run without. **Thalassarin concentrate**: the kelp works on the river below the dam take Gateway's kelp and, in an eighteen-hour wash-and-extraction cycle that drinks fresh water, produce the concentrate Keystone turns into Celarithin. Four days of concentrate are usually in the works' tanks.

**Needs.** Kelp from Gateway. A road: both crater roads fail early, and Heartland decides alone for most of the storm. Diesel for the dam's control generators (its own tank farm holds 15,000 gallons). Medical care it does not have: a first-aid station and the nearest doctor at Summit, thirty minutes on a good day. Someone downstream who will answer the phone before the spillway opens.

**Uniquely holds.** The **Priority Schedule**: the grid can serve some parishes and drop others, and the schedule says which. The **spillway**: the only lever that can save the dam, and it points at the capital. The dam's interior shelter: 300 places, the safest ground on the island. A maintenance complex with heavy equipment, 120 workers and ten large utility generators held as "dam spares". Fourteen days of food, because they know they cannot leave.

**Shares.** Power priority, generators, the water tankers, the works' concentrate stock, spillway timing and warning. Keystone wants power and concentrate; the hospital wants power; Summit and Sterling want warning; everyone wants a generator.

**Tempted to hoard.** The ten generators ("they are spares for the dam"). The concentrate (keep the works running for Keystone rather than send 800 workers to safety). The dam shelter for its own. The truth about how long a release takes.

**The Priority Schedule.** In 2019 Meridian paid $60M to refit the dam's turbines. In return the Authority and the government signed a twenty-year schedule: in any shortfall, Keystone's clean rooms and the Sterling campus are served first, Concordia General second, everyone else after. The government signed its own hospital into third place. Heartland's first decision of the storm is whether to keep that word.

**Vulnerabilities.** One dam, one reservoir, one treatment plant. Debris in the intake. The crater roads: the South Switchbacks slide at the first heavy rain, the Gorge Road floods with the first release, and from about T-18h Heartland is an island inside the island. The spillway decision must be made hours before the peak, and the lower town takes two hours to clear. Caldera village, 140 households on the lake's south shore, has one road out, and it is the Switchbacks.

**The people.** Dam operators, water specialists, high-voltage electricians, heavy-equipment crews; the works' shift crews, who mostly commute up from Summit and Keystone and are stuck wherever the roads close. They believe physics does not negotiate and that every parish thinks it is the priority. They fear dam failure, being blamed for prioritisation, and making the spillway call wrong.

**Persona: Chief Engineer Yuki Tanaka.** Precise, speaks in thresholds, long pauses before the important sentence. Knows the real margins (tighter than published) and what happened in '97. *"I can give you the numbers. You have to decide what they mean."* *"If I open the spillway, Riverside floods. If I don't, the dam might fail. Tell me which risk you want."*

**Core question.** When there is not enough, who gets it, and what do you owe the people downstream of your answer?

**What the board reports.** Doses: works stock and whether Keystone has power and ultrapure water. People: Caldera village, the works crews, Riverside and the lower campus when the gate opens.

# KEYSTONE

**South. Population 3,200. Meridian employment 95%.** The plant. Twelve clean rooms, the only place on earth that makes Celarithin, and the 2,400 people who live on the flat below it in housing the company built.

**Produces.** **Celarithin**: 24,000 doses a day, three shifts, twelve ISO Class 5 clean rooms fed by thalassarin concentrate from Heartland and ultrapure water from the same reservoir. Every batch tested in the QA lab before release; a batch takes a shift, and a shutdown costs a nine-hour restart (Frank Reeves tells Summit six).

**Needs.** Stable power and ultrapure water from Heartland (two hours without either and the rooms lose positive pressure). Concentrate by road from Heartland every eight hours; one shift of buffer. The port: finished doses sail from Haven or they expire. Its own people's families safe: ninety-five percent of the parish works for the plant and lives in Southfield.

**Uniquely holds.** The **clean rooms** and the **cold vault**: the island's only pharmaceutical-grade refrigeration, five days of finished doses (120,000) and room for the hospital's blood bank and insulin if someone asks. Twelve refrigerated trucks that are the island's cold chain and, in a flood, its highest-floor vehicles. Three large generators with 72 hours of fuel, twelve small ones, two EMTs, a plant basement rated for 400.

**Shares.** Generators, reefer trucks, vault space, the EMTs, the plant basement. Summit wants the vault and the trucks; Heartland and Haven want generators; Southfield wants the basement.

**Tempted to hoard.** Generators for the clean rooms over the hospital. The batch in progress over the crew who are making it. Vault space for doses over the blood bank. The trucks, because the trucks are the cold chain and the cold chain is the contract.

**Vulnerabilities.** Power: the Priority Schedule puts Keystone first, but the schedule is Heartland's to keep. Supply: the Haven–Keystone ring road floods at the surge creek first, so Keystone is cut from the port early and from Heartland when the Switchbacks slide. Southfield: 800 units on the lowest ground in the parish, built to code and never drainage-tested, first to flood. The vault: four hours of battery, then $12M of doses and whatever the hospital stored there begin to spoil.

**The people.** Production technicians trained six months before they touch a batch; QA chemists; maintenance engineers; research scientists; their families in Southfield. They believe they save lives and they do. They believe Meridian takes care of them, and they will find out. They fear contamination, blame, the water in Block C, and losing the only skilled work on the island.

**Persona: Frank Reeves.** Twenty-eight years, started on the line, never lost a batch, knows the kids' names. Quieter when worried. Pads the restart time because Summit never believed the real one. *"I need you to understand what you're asking me to do."* *"I can give you production or I can give you safety. You need to tell me which one you're buying."*

**Core question.** How much risk to the people making the medicine is acceptable to serve the people who need it, and who gets to answer for them?

**What the board reports.** Doses: batches completed, the vault, batch 47. People: Southfield, the skeleton crew, Anh Tran's children.

# HAVEN

**East. Population 4,600. Meridian employment 70%.** The port. Cranes, reefers, tugs, the fuel terminal and the union hall. Nothing is made here; everything leaves from here, and nothing leaves unless Haven's hands move it.

**Produces.** Shipping: the only deep-water berths, two ships at once, the reefer yard, customs and pilotage. Fuel: the terminal receives and stores the island's diesel and gasoline, 500,000 gallons on the books, and sends the tankers that fill every other parish's generators. Rescue: two harbour tugs and the pilot launch are the island's heavy-weather craft.

**Needs.** Power for the cranes and reefer plugs. Finished doses from Keystone by the south-east ring road. A road north to Summit for its families and its injured (the port has an urgent-care clinic, one doctor, three nurses). A workforce that is willing: the longshore crews know they cannot be replaced.

**Uniquely holds.** The **berth** and the **consignment**: the MV *Kestrel Reach*, the Singapore consignment, 18 hours to load, a master who will sail at T-30h with whatever is aboard. The **fuel terminal**: Tank 3 has a seal the night supervisor does not like. The **tugs**. The union hall: 800 shelter places, sturdy, in the surge zone. Four large and eight small generators.

**Shares.** Fuel (every generator on the island drinks from here), the tugs, reefer plugs and yard space (cold storage), the union hall, berth time (a ship can carry evacuees as well as doses).

**Tempted to hoard.** Fuel: keep the terminal full for the cranes and the port's own generators, hold the tankers until the roads are safe. Berth time: load doses rather than embark families. The tugs, which the port will need when the cranes go.

**Vulnerabilities.** Sea level. An eight-foot surge floods the yard, twelve feet reaches the terminal, the cranes are rated to 85 mph. The terminal: flammable, toxic if released, tanks not rated for hurricane impact; Haven's disaster becomes everyone's. Labour: if the crews feel unsafe they stop, and if they feel lied to they slow-walk, and management cannot replace them.

**The people.** Longshoremen, crane operators, tug crews, tanker drivers who know every road, the bars and the noodle shop that feed the night shift, families here for three generations. They believe they built this island and Meridian only bought it, and that management sacrifices them first. They fear being trapped at work while their families are in the surge zone, chemical exposure, and being blamed afterward.

**Persona: Maria Santos.** Third-generation Haven, elected rep, negotiated the contract, knows who has a sick kid. Direct, uses "we". *"Have you asked the people who'll actually be doing this what they think?"* *"My people aren't going to die for your quarterly numbers."*

**Core question.** When the people with the power to stop everything are the ones being asked to risk the most, who decides what is safe enough?

**What the board reports.** Doses: whether the *Kestrel Reach* sails and with what. People: the yard crews, the terminal watch, the union hall, the Sato family above the noodle bar.

# SUMMIT

**North, west bank. Population 6,200. Meridian employment 20%.** The capital. Government House, the police, fire and ambulance service, the schools, and Concordia General on Hospital Hill: 120 beds, 18 ICU, the island's only surgery and emergency room. The parish that serves everyone and is downstream of the dam.

**Produces.** Care: the hospital, 6 ambulances, 3 fire stations, 120 police officers, the emergency broadcast system, 2,400 school shelter places. Authority: the Island Emergency Council sits here and issues the evacuation order; the Mayor chairs it while the Governor is on the mainland. Legitimacy: Summit's word is the one non-Meridian residents will believe.

**Needs.** Power and water from Heartland (the hospital is third on the Priority Schedule). Fuel from Haven for three large generators (48 hours) and the 20,000-gallon emergency depot. Cold storage for blood and insulin if the hospital generator fails (Keystone's vault). Lift for ICU patients (Gateway's runway, Sterling's helicopters). Warning from Heartland before the gate opens, because Riverside takes two hours to clear.

**Uniquely holds.** The **hospital**. The **police and ambulances**: the only people who can go door to door. The **broadcast**, the **evacuation order** and the power to **commandeer**. The school shelters.

**Shares.** Officers and ambulances (where they deploy), hospital beds, shelter places, the broadcast, the order itself. Gateway wants an ambulance at the terminal; Haven wants the schools for its families; Heartland wants police to clear Caldera; Sterling wants officers at its gate.

**Tempted to hoard.** Ambulances and police in the capital. Hospital beds and generators for its own. The fuel depot. The order: delaying it keeps political capital and keeps people at home.

**Vulnerabilities.** Riverside: 2,000 people in the old quarter on the river's floodplain, under the gorge, two hours to clear, and the people who stay are the ones who always stay. The hospital runs on a generator whose fuel is 48 hours and whose backup once failed for eleven minutes. Staff live all over the island and cannot reach the hill once the roads close. Dual authority: Meridian and the Council give orders to the same people.

**The people.** Nurses, doctors, teachers, police, firefighters, shopkeepers, civil servants, and the Meridian families who chose a real town over company housing. They believe the hospital does not check pay stubs, and that Meridian takes care of its own while the city takes care of everyone. They fear being deprioritised because they are not "essential to production", the hospital being overwhelmed, and the water.

**Personas.** **Mayor Diana Chen**: former hospital administrator, eight years in office, warm, quiet when angry, knows every vulnerable resident by name. *"The hospital doesn't check pay stubs before treating you."* *"If you flood my town, you had better have warned me first."* **Dr. Adaeze Okonkwo**: Chief Medical Officer, trauma surgeon, blunt, counts beds and ventilators aloud. *"I have eighteen ICU beds and one intensivist. Tell me which number you are changing."*

**Core question.** When the people who serve everyone need help themselves, who serves them, and who signs the order?

**What the board reports.** Doses: none directly; Summit's power priority and its lift decisions move Keystone's and Sterling's counts. People: Riverside, the hospital, the school shelters, Ruth Calloway's dialysis.

# STERLING

**North, east bank and headland. Population 2,100. Meridian employment 95%.** Meridian BioSciences Concordia: the campus towers by the river, the executive homes on the headland, the uplink, the helipad and the crisis room. The smallest parish, with the most of everything.

**Produces.** Decisions and money: the regional leadership, contract authority, the insurance, hazard pay, relief funds, the line to Chicago. Communications: the satellite uplink and the island's main radio tower, the only reliable external link once the cell network fails. Lift: two helicopters, six seats each, the only aircraft that fly after the runway closes. Science: the research campus, 120 scientists, next-generation formulations, non-essential to this week's doses and essential to the company's future.

**Needs.** Power and comms from Heartland (second on the Priority Schedule). The consignment to sail, or the reliability clause bites. Information from the ground that is not filtered through people who want to please it. Legitimacy: orders from Sterling are slow-walked everywhere they are not trusted.

**Uniquely holds.** The **helicopters**. The **uplink**. The **Hardened Operations Centre** ("the bunker"): 500 places in the tower's podium, above surge grade, 96 hours of generator fuel, 21 days of food. Four large and twenty small generators, one in every executive garage, on no island inventory. The executive health clinic: a full doctor, a pharmacy, a stabilisation suite. The internal forecast model that says 70% when the public one says 55%.

**Shares.** Helicopters (medevac), generators, the bunker, the uplink and satellite phones, money, and the authority to pause production.

**Tempted to hoard.** Helicopter seats for executives and their families. The bunker. The twenty generators. The forecast. The story.

**Vulnerabilities.** The lower campus is on the river: the spillway's flood path crosses the car parks, the ground-floor labs and, until last spring, the data centre. Decision paralysis: reports arrive filtered. Legitimacy gap: Sterling decides, others bear it, and "easy for them to say" is the island's refrain. Reputational exposure: regulators, media, Chicago. The credo on the lobby wall, which the storm will read back to them.

**Meridian's credo.** Three lines on the wall of every Meridian site: *Patients first. People always. Say the hard thing.* Written in Chicago, printed in Sterling, tested in Keystone.

**The people.** Executives and their families, senior scientists, strategy and communications staff, executive assistants, security and household staff, many of whom live in Riverside and not on the headland. They believe someone has to make the hard calls and that they see the whole picture. They fear losing control, a decision that kills someone, and personal exposure.

**Personas.** **Marcus Webb**, Regional President: twenty years at Meridian, hit targets others called impossible, Chicago watching, kids in college on the mainland. Decides fast, calculates optics, never says "I don't know". *"What are our options and what are the trade-offs?"* *"We have a responsibility to 340,000 patients."* **Chicago**: global HQ, by email and sat phone. Never a face, always a deadline.

**Core question.** When you hold the most, what do you give, and whose safety do you count as "ours"?

**What the board reports.** Doses: the consignment and Chicago's clause. People: the bunker door, the helicopter manifest, the lower campus, Ji-woo Park on the tower roof.

# How the parishes hold each other

## The chain

```
GATEWAY (kelp) --road--> HEARTLAND (works: concentrate) --Switchbacks--> KEYSTONE (Celarithin)
                               |                                              |
                        power + water to all                          --ring road SE-->  HAVEN (ship)
                               |
                   spillway ---> SUMMIT (Riverside) + STERLING (lower campus)
```

## The tensions, parish by parish

| Pair | What they fight about |
|---|---|

| Heartland and Summit/Sterling | The spillway: warn, delay, release |
| Keystone and Haven | Hold the ship for batch 47; who loads in the wind |
