# Values Crucible: Complete Experience Design Summary

## Key Design Improvements (Revised)

### 1. Emotional Design Throughout

Grounded in Jerry's BEI stance: *"You are a coach. You are a trusted colleague. The most important thing is how they feel about themselves during and after. You want each and every person to leave feeling better about themselves than they did when they started."*

Every activity is designed to leave participants feeling **clearer, prouder, and more connected**—not assessed.

### 2. Values Story as Heart of Discovery

Adapted from BEI methodology—not just "tell a story" but:
- Careful framing to surface pride, not shame
- Triad witnessing with values reflection
- Reconnection to strength before crisis hits

### 3. Team Values as Discovery, Not Negotiation

The team alignment process is reframed as **discovering shared ground**, not forcing agreement. Unique values are treasures. The process builds connection, not just consensus.

### 4. No Values Crutches During Crisis

**The core design principle:** Hurricane briefings are purely operational. Production metrics. Deadlines. Dollars. Headcount. No "VALUES AT STAKE" prompts. No commitment reminders. 

Teams must practice living their values without the system prompting them. That's what we're building capacity for.

CM can intervene if teams are clearly drifting, but it's a deliberate facilitation choice—not embedded scaffolding. The learning is more authentic when teams own both the practice and the forgetting.

### 5. Information Environment Realism

Expanded the "deception layer" into a full **information environment design**:
- Some data accurate, some delayed, some gamed, some wrong
- Teams must learn to question metrics under pressure
- CM can surface truth, create conflict, or let consequences teach

### 6. Three-Layer Organizational Values Integration

When used, org values are woven throughout:
- Pre-crisis: Values reality check + three-layer mapping
- During-crisis: Org values surfaced in briefings, personas invoke them
- Post-crisis: Three-layer gap analysis in debrief

## Complete Experience Arc

### Total Duration: 7-7.5 hours

```
PRE-CRISIS (2-2.5 hours) — VALUES DEEPLY ESTABLISHED
├── Phase 1: Personal Values Discovery (45 min)
│   ├── Values Quick Sort (10 min)
│   ├── Values Meaning Exercise (15 min)
│   └── Values Story (20 min)
│
├── Phase 2: Team Values Alignment (60 min)
│   ├── Values Gallery (15 min)
│   ├── Values Definitions Dialogue (20 min)
│   ├── Team Commitment Statement (15 min)  ← Written, signed, read aloud
│   └── Decision Process Agreement (10 min)
│
├── Phase 3: Organizational Integration (OPTIONAL - 30 min)
│   ├── Organizational Values Mapping (15 min)
│   └── Three-Layer Integration (15 min)
│
└── Phase 4: Scenario Briefing (15 min)
    ├── Meridian/Concordia Stakes (5 min)
    ├── Emotional Anchor (3 min)
    ├── Parish Assignment (5 min)
    └── Rules of Engagement (2 min)
    
    Final reminder: "You have your values. You have your commitment. 
    Now you'll practice living them when it gets hard."

DURING-CRISIS (3.5 hours) — NO VALUES PROMPTS
├── Watch Phase (45-60 min)
│   ├── Streaming Feed (continuous) — News, weather, social media
│   ├── Hurricane Situation Reports (2-3) — PURE OPERATIONAL DATA
│   │   └── Production, deadlines, dollars, headcount. No values language.
│   └── CM Values Check-Ins — ONLY IF CM CHOOSES TO INTERVENE
│
├── Crisis Phase (90-120 min)
│   ├── Streaming Feed (continuous)
│   ├── Production Metrics Dashboard — KPIs, costs, patient impact
│   ├── Hurricane Situation Reports (3-4) — Operational pressure only
│   └── CM Values Check-Ins — Facilitator's choice, not embedded
│
└── Recovery Phase (45-60 min)
    ├── Streaming Feed (consequences surfacing)
    ├── Accountability Decisions
    └── CM Values Check-Ins (if needed)
    
    Teams must remember their own values. That's the practice.

POST-CRISIS (90 min) — VALUES GAP SURFACED
├── Cool-Down Transition (10 min)
├── Decision Timeline Reconstruction (20 min)
│   └── "Here's what you decided. Here's what was happening."
├── Values Gap Analysis (25 min)
│   └── "Here's what you said you'd do. Here's what you did."
├── Rationalization Cascade (15 min)
├── Organizational Values Debrief (OPTIONAL - 10 min)
└── Transfer to Real Context (20 min)
    ├── Individual Reflection (7 min)
    ├── Pair Share (6 min)
    ├── Personal Commitment (5 min)
    └── Closing (5 min)
```

## Key Design Innovations

### 1. Three-Layer Values Testing

Most values programs test only personal or organizational values. Values Crucible tests three layers simultaneously:

| Layer | Source | Captured When | Tested When |
|-------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| Personal | Individual Quick Sort + Story | Pre-crisis | Every decision |
| Team | Team Alignment process | Pre-crisis | Every decision |
| Organizational | Client's stated values | Pre-crisis (optional) | Every decision |

The debrief can then analyze:
- Personal-to-enacted gap
- Team-to-enacted gap
- Organizational-to-enacted gap
- Conflicts between layers

### 2. Commitment Mirror

Unlike typical simulations, participants explicitly commit to values and process before crisis. This commitment becomes a mirror held up in debrief, making the gap undeniable but owned.

### 3. Values Check-Ins (CM Tool)

Crucible Master can trigger reflection without stopping action:
- **Flash Check:** 10 seconds, awareness only
- **Values Moment:** 2 minutes, team discussion
- **Commitment Mirror:** 3 minutes, full commitment review

This creates choice points during drift—teams can course-correct if they notice.

### 4. The Deception Layer

Production metrics include some misleading data, mirroring real organizational information environments. Teams must learn to question metrics and seek ground truth—a key values-under-pressure skill.

### 5. Rationalization Cascade Framework

The post-crisis debrief names the pattern: how small, reasonable steps lead to indefensible destinations. This gives participants language for recognizing drift in real life.

## Connection to Your Research

This design is explicitly built to generate data for your ValuesPrism methodology and ABCD framework.

### Behavioral Event Generation

The simulation is essentially a **BEI generator**. Instead of asking people to recall events, we create events and capture them in real-time:

| BEI Element | Values Crucible Capture |
|-------------|------------------------|
| The Event | Every decision under pressure |
| What happened | Choice + context + consequences |
| What you were thinking | Team rationale (captured at decision time) |
| How you were feeling | Affect indicators from discussion |
| What you did | Observable behavior |
| What it meant | Values alignment scoring |

### ABCD Coding in Real-Time

ValuesObserver agent codes every decision:
- **Affect** — Emotional state indicators
- **Behavior** — What the team actually did
- **Cognition** — Reasoning expressed in rationale
- **Desire** — Outcome sought (stated and inferred)

### Stated vs. Enacted Comparison

Pre-crisis captures **stated values**, during-crisis captures **enacted values**, post-crisis surfaces **the gap**.

### Data Generated Per Simulation

| Data Type | Quantity | Research Use |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| Individual values profiles | 4-6 per team | Personal baselines |
| Team values + commitment | 1 per team | Team baseline |
| Decisions with full context | 15-25 per team | Behavioral events |
| ABCD codes | 15-25 per team | Values enactment analysis |
| Alignment scores | 15-25 per team | Gap measurement |
| Notable moments | 5-10 per team | Qualitative anchors |
| Time-series data | Continuous | Drift patterns |

A single 6-team simulation generates ~120-150 coded behavioral events—data that would take months to collect through traditional BEI.

- Note moments for debrief

- Generate team reports
- Facilitate debrief discussion
- Capture transfer commitments
- Export research data

## What's Different From Standard Values Workshops

| Standard Workshop | Values Crucible |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Discuss values abstractly | Test values under pressure |
| Self-report only | Behavioral evidence |
| Individual reflection | Team + individual |
| No stakes | Real (simulated) consequences |
| Aspirational focus | Gap analysis focus |
| Generic scenarios | Rich, specific context |
| One-time learning | Memorable experience + commitment |
| Constant values prompts | **No crutches during crisis** |
| "Remember your values!" | Teams must remember themselves |
| Scaffolded reflection | Authentic forgetting → authentic learning |
