# Decision: the RTS flow as adult learning, two teams per parish, five parishes

Decided by Jerry Wolf in a grilling session on 2026-10-21 (19:28 to 20:13), held through decision cards in the thread "Audit fix spec and plan". Jerry asked to grill the RTS design "instead of just accepting what we have already", and to look at the flow from an andragogical (adult learning) perspective. The design doc [[design-2026-10-21-rts-godot]] is revised to match.

## The learning flow

1. **Shape.** A large values build-up at the front, a Mission brief after each quarter, and a final debrief (Jerry: "Big build up of values on the front end. Building a real values stance. Then huddles, 'Mission briefs', at the end of each phase. Final debrief at the end."). This gives the do, reflect, try again loop four times instead of once.
2. **The values stance: me, then us, then the trade-off** (about 20 minutes).
   - Each person picks three values on their phone and tells a 60-second story about a time one of them cost them something.
   - The team agrees on three values, ranks them for when they collide, and signs a one-line statement.
   - Moments can cite the ranking ("You put COURAGE above SAFETY"). The debrief compares three layers: what I said, what we said, and what we did.
3. **Time: 120 minutes by default, editable by the Crucible master (CM).**

   | Segment | Minutes |
   |---|---|
   | Front end: stance (20) plus parish brief and practice (10) | 30 |
   | Four quarters of 14 minutes | 56 |
   | Three Mission briefs of 4 minutes, after Q1, Q2 and Q3 | 12 |
   | Final debrief | 22 |

   The CM sets every length in setup and can stretch or cut a Mission brief live. Every change is logged, so replay still matches.
4. **Mission brief: look back, then brief forward.** The storm pauses.
   - 1.5 minutes: a replay of the quarter, facts only, and one question on the team's ranked values.
   - 2.5 minutes: a situation report on the next quarter, and one if-then intent pinned to the tablet.
   - The next quarter notes when that intent is tested. The debrief shows whether it held.
   - The CM can edit the questions.
5. **What players are told: the why, not the scale.** The CM says that the island remembers what you do, not what you say, and that nothing about values is scored during play. The seven anchors appear only in the debrief.
6. **Bridge to their jobs: none built in.** Jerry: "it is more about the scenario resonating with them." No industry packs and no "where does this happen at work" step. The story has to land on its own, through residents with names and faces and stakes that feel like leadership.
7. **Safety: each person's record is theirs alone.** A person's stance, their votes, and the moments where the team overrode them show only on their own phone in the debrief. The team sees vote counts, never names.
8. **After the session.** Still open: what happens to the private commitment. Recommended: a reminder the person saves to their own calendar for 30 days later, with nothing sent to anyone.

## Teams, power and the win

9. **Two teams per parish.** In each parish, a community team speaks for the local people and a Meridian team for the company's interests. There is one parish and one set of units, and both teams vote on every decision.
10. **Whoever owns the resource decides.** Jerry: "the engine creates situations that force the choice to use resources."
    - **Community resources** have breadth: many smaller units. Both teams vote, and the community team makes the final decision.
    - **Meridian resources** have depth: fewer, far bigger assets. The reverse applies, and the Meridian team decides.
    - **Moments that need both:** each team decides its own part and sees the other team's vote live.
    - The aim is a back-and-forth between the two teams, and conflict.
11. **The win: the team with the most resources at the end wins.** Jerry: "There should be a clear push to preserve resources... Create the tension." Spending resources to save people costs a team the win. This reverses the round-2 audit gap ("hoarding can win") into a designed temptation. The resource total is the game's score, not a values score; value readings stay hidden during play. Witness, not judge, still holds for values.
12. **Standings are public.** Every team's resource total shows live on the wall and again at each Mission brief. The winner is crowned at the end, and the debrief puts the people lost beside the crown.
13. **Resources between parishes: gift, loan or trade.** The owner picks one at send time.
    - A gift moves the resource to the receiving team's standing for good.
    - A loan returns if the storm spares it.
    - A trade is an offer on the map that the other side accepts, and both sides move together.
    - Spoken promises are not enforced. The kind of send is logged and read.
14. **Ties.** Each team picks a rep in the front end, and the rep breaks 3–3 splits. The role passes to someone new each quarter, and tie-breaks are logged.
15. **Pressure comes from authority.** A push counts as pressure, and so can read Capitulating (−2), only when it comes from a team with authority over the team being pushed:
    - Meridian HQ (the centre Meridian team) pushing another Meridian team;
    - Sterling's government ordering a community.

    Pushes between peers are logged as persuasion. This replaces the audit rule that only off-board voices apply pressure. It follows the dissertation: pressure comes from those with power over you.

## Five parishes (amends canon v3)

16. **Five parishes, four on the coast and one in the centre.** Each has a wealth of both community and Meridian resources.
    - **Centre:** Heartland and Summit merge. It holds Meridian HQ and the dam, which is a Meridian asset built on the community's behalf. Its name is not yet chosen.
    - **North:** Sterling becomes the capital, the hospital and the government, and fills the whole north tile.
    - **Gateway, Keystone and Haven** are unchanged.
17. **Ten teams.** Five parishes with two teams each.
    - About 60 people at the nurses session, so 6 per team.
    - About 30 at Penn CLO in March 2027, so 3 per team.

    The earlier idea of 12 teams is replaced.

## Consequences

- [[design-2026-10-21-rts-godot]] is revised: the session, map, units, teams, moments, readings, debrief and screens sections all change.
- [[concordia-parishes-v3]], [[values-crucible-session-90]] and the `crucible-*-v3.md` drafts need a v4 pass for five parishes and two teams; content stays Jerry's call.
- Open:
  - the centre parish's name;
  - the post-session commitment;
  - which parish teams start with which resources.
