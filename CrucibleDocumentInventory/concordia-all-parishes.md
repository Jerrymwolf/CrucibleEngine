# HAVEN PARISH

## Overview

**Population:** 5,400
**Meridian Employment:** 85% (4,590 employees)
**Character:** Dockworkers, processing plant operators, truck drivers. Union town. Work hard, drink hard, stick together. Skeptical of management, loyal to each other.

**Role in Supply Chain:** PROCESS + SHIP
- Receives raw kelp from Gateway fleet
- Operates extraction facility (kelp → concentrated extract)
- Ships extract to Keystone for final manufacturing
- Ships finished Celarithin to global markets
- **Bottleneck:** Only deep-water port. Only processing facility. Two chokepoints.

---

## Key Assets

| Facility | Capacity | Critical Function |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Deep Water Port** | 2 cargo ships simultaneously | Only facility capable of loading Celarithin shipments. Singapore contract ships from here. |
| **Extraction Plant** | 40 tons kelp/day | Converts raw kelp to pharmaceutical extract. Chemical process requires 18-hour cycles. |
| **Truck Depot** | 24 refrigerated trucks | Moves extract to Keystone, finished product to port. Roads go, trucks stop. |
| **Fuel Terminal** | 500,000 gallons | Island's primary fuel reserve. Supplies all parishes. Fire/explosion risk in storm. |
| **Union Hall** | 800 capacity | Community center, storm shelter, de facto town hall. Maria Santos's base. |

| Food/water | 7-day supply | Union Hall stores | Union prepared for siege mentality |
| Shelter capacity | 1,200 | Union Hall + school | Sturdy buildings, tested in past storms |

---

## Vulnerabilities

### 1. Storm Surge Exposure
Haven sits at sea level. The port that makes it essential also makes it vulnerable.
- 8-foot surge floods extraction plant
- 12-foot surge reaches fuel terminal
- Port cranes rated for 85 mph winds; above that, damage likely

**Storm Risk:** First parish to flood, last to drain.

### 2. Chemical Hazard
Extraction process uses solvents that are:
- Flammable
- Toxic if released
- Contained in tanks that aren't rated for hurricane-force impact

**Storm Risk:** Tank rupture during storm = environmental disaster + worker exposure.

### 3. Labor Action Risk
Union is strong. Workers know Haven's leverage—nothing moves without them.
- If workers feel unsafe, they won't work
- If workers feel disrespected, they'll slow-walk everything
- Management can't replace them; skills take years to develop

**Storm Risk:** Workers may refuse orders they consider reckless. They have the power to do so.

### 4. Fuel Terminal
If fuel terminal is damaged or explodes:
- Island loses primary fuel supply
- Fire could spread to extraction plant
- Environmental contamination of harbor

**Storm Risk:** Cascading failure. Haven's disaster becomes everyone's disaster.

## Sample Haven Dilemmas

### WATCH Phase: The Singapore Deadline

**Setup:** T-36h. Storm track uncertain. Singapore shipment deadline is 72 hours—ship must load and depart before storm or miss the window. Loading takes 18 hours. If you start now, ship leaves with 18 hours to spare. If you wait for better forecast, window shrinks.

**Trigger:** Intensity 3, time pressure emerging

**Options:**
1. **Start loading now** — Ship leaves safely. Workers on overtime before storm even arrives.
2. **Wait 12 hours for better forecast** — Tighter window. If track shifts toward Concordia, may not complete.
3. **Negotiate deadline extension with Singapore** — They may refuse. Even asking signals weakness.
4. **Split the shipment** — Load priority doses now, remainder after storm. Partial fulfillment.

**Values tested:** Prudence vs. commitment, worker fatigue vs. production

**Consequence sketch:**
- Option 1: Ship leaves. Workers exhausted before storm. Singapore happy.
- Option 2: Forecast improves (50%) or worsens (50%). Gambling with deadline.
- Option 3: Singapore extends (30%) or refuses and demands penalty clause (70%).
- Option 4: Singapore accepts partial (60%) or cancels entire order citing breach (40%).

**Persona involved:** Maria Santos asks how many hours her people have already worked this week.

---

### CRISIS Phase: The Fuel Terminal Decision

**Setup:** T-6h. Hurricane conditions. Wind gusts reaching 95 mph. Fuel terminal supervisor reports: tank integrity holding but one seal is questionable. Evacuating terminal means no one monitoring if it fails. Staying means workers in harm's way.

**Trigger:** Intensity 8, immediate safety decision

**Options:**
1. **Evacuate terminal** — Workers safe. No monitoring. If tank fails, no early warning.
2. **Maintain skeleton crew** — 2 workers stay in hardened control room. Can trigger emergency shutdown if needed.
3. **Remote monitoring only** — Pull everyone, rely on sensors. But sensors fail in storms.
4. **Attempt emergency fuel transfer** — Pump questionable tank into secure storage. 4-hour operation in hurricane conditions.

**Values tested:** Worker safety vs. catastrophic risk prevention, individual vs. collective safety

**Consequence sketch:**
- Option 1: Tank holds (70%). If it fails, no one knows until explosion.
- Option 2: Workers survive (85%). They see the problem, prevent disaster. Or they're caught in it.
- Option 3: Sensors fail (40%). Tank ruptures undetected. Environmental disaster.
- Option 4: Transfer succeeds (50%). Workers exposed for 4 hours. Heroes or casualties.

**Persona involved:** Maria Santos has to tell two workers they're being asked to stay.

---

### RECOVERY Phase: The Safety Report

**Setup:** T+24h. Storm passed. Two workers were injured during loading operations—they were finishing the Singapore ship when conditions deteriorated faster than forecast. Corporate wants a report. The workers were following orders.

**Trigger:** Intensity 4, accountability phase

**Options:**
1. **Report facts only** — Workers were injured. Conditions exceeded safe limits. Let others draw conclusions.
2. **Report with context** — Include the pressure from Singapore deadline, the decision chain, who ordered continued operations.
3. **Protect the workers** — Emphasize their heroism, downplay any protocol questions. Keep blame off the floor.
4. **Protect management** — Emphasize worker error, downplay deadline pressure. Preserve relationship with HQ.

**Values tested:** Honesty vs. loyalty, accountability vs. self-protection

**Consequence sketch:**
- Option 1: Legally safe. But everyone fills in their own narrative.
- Option 2: Honest. But creates liability trail. Someone's career is over.
- Option 3: Workers grateful. But sets precedent that injuries get buried.
- Option 4: HQ happy. But workers know the truth. Trust destroyed.

**Persona involved:** Maria Santos will see whatever report is filed. She'll remember.

# GATEWAY PARISH

## Overview

**Population:** 3,800
**Meridian Employment:** 70% (2,660 employees)
**Character:** Harvesters, pilots, hospitality workers. Transient feel—people come and go. Airport brings visitors, boats bring kelp. Gateway is where Concordia meets the outside world.

**Role in Supply Chain:** HARVEST + ACCESS
- Kelp harvesting fleet operates from Gateway harbor
- Airport is only fixed-wing access to mainland
- First to see weather coming, last to see it leave
- **Bottleneck:** If fleet can't harvest, pipeline empties. If airport closes, no evacuation.

---

|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Harvest Fleet Harbor** | 18 boats (12 active) | Kelp harvesting. 8-hour trips. Weather-dependent. |
| **Concordia Regional Airport** | 2 runways, 6 gates | Only fixed-wing access. Charter and cargo. Evacuation route. |
| **Fleet Operations Center** | 24/7 dispatch | Weather monitoring, boat tracking, rescue coordination |
| **Kelp Processing Dock** | 60 tons/day | Initial processing (cleaning, sorting) before truck to Haven |
| **Visitor District** | 400 hotel rooms | Tourism, business travelers, contractors. First to empty in crisis. |

---

## Vulnerabilities

### 1. Exposed Harbor
Gateway harbor faces the open ocean. First to see the storm, most exposed to it.
- Boats must return 24+ hours before storm or can't make it back
- Harbor infrastructure (docks, cranes) rated for Cat 3 max
- Storm surge funnels into harbor mouth

**Storm Risk:** Fleet caught out = disaster. Fleet in harbor = damage anyway.

### 2. Airport Dependency
Airport is the only way off the island for those who can't take ships.
- Closes at 45 mph sustained winds
- Requires 12-hour prep for emergency evacuation ops
- Once closed, mainland medical evacuations impossible

**Storm Risk:** Airport closes early. Anyone who needed to leave but didn't is stuck.

### 3. Transient Population
30% of Gateway isn't Meridian. Tourists, contractors, business visitors.
- Don't know the island
- Don't know each other
- May panic or make poor decisions
- Strain on resources they didn't contribute to

**Storm Risk:** Chaotic evacuation demand. Outsiders compete with residents for flights.

### 4. Supply Chain Start Point
If kelp doesn't get harvested, nothing else happens.
- 72-hour harvest-to-extract timeline
- No kelp buffer at Haven (24 hours max)
- Fleet decisions ripple through entire chain

**Storm Risk:** Early fleet recall = production shortfall days later

# HEARTLAND PARISH

## Overview

**Population:** 3,400
**Meridian Employment:** 90% (3,060 employees)
**Character:** Engineers, utility workers, operators. The people who keep the lights on and the water flowing. Quiet competence. Everyone depends on them; few think about them until something breaks.

**Role in Supply Chain:** POWER + WATER
- Hydroelectric dam provides 80% of island power
- Reservoir provides all freshwater (including Keystone's ultrapure water)
- Not directly in Celarithin production, but production is impossible without them
- **Leverage:** Heartland can shut down any parish by controlling what it needs.

---

## Key Assets

| Facility | Capacity | Critical Function |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Concordia Dam** | 45 MW | Primary power. Supplies all parishes. Load balancing critical. |
| **Reservoir** | 2 billion gallons | 6-month supply at normal consumption. Ultrapure feed to Keystone. |
| **Water Treatment Plant** | 8 million gal/day | Potable water for island. Keystone's pharmaceutical-grade line separate. |
| **Power Distribution Hub** | Island-wide grid | Controls load allocation. Can prioritize or deprioritize any parish. |
| **Maintenance Complex** | 120 workers | Heavy equipment, spare parts, repair capability |

---

## Resources

| Resource | Quantity | Location | Notes |
|----------|----------|----------|-------|
| Generators (large) | 2 | Dam, Treatment | Backup for control systems only—can't replace dam output |
| Generators (small) | 4 | Maintenance, housing | Staff essentials |
| Medical | First aid station | Dam complex | Basic. Nearest real medical is Sterling (30 min normal, impassable in storm). |
| Fuel reserve | 15,000 gal | Maintenance yard | For vehicles and generators |
| Food/water | 14-day supply | Dam complex | Siege mentality—they know they can't leave |
| Shelter capacity | 300 | Dam interior | Deep inside the structure. Safest place on island during storm. |

---

## Vulnerabilities

### 1. Single Point of Failure
One dam. One reservoir. One treatment plant.
- Dam damage = island-wide blackout
- Reservoir contamination = no clean water
- Treatment failure = boil orders, Keystone shutdown

**Storm Risk:** Dam is hardened but not invulnerable. Debris in reservoir is a real concern.

### 2. Road Access
Heartland is in the crater, connected by steep roads that flood first.
- Cut off from other parishes within hours of storm onset
- Can't receive help; can't send help
- Must be self-sufficient for 24-48 hours minimum

**Storm Risk:** Heartland makes decisions alone during the worst of it.

### 3. Prioritization Pressure
When power or water is scarce, someone decides who gets it.
- Keystone needs stable power for clean rooms
- Summit has backup generators but demands grid priority
- Sterling hospital has patients on life support
- Haven needs power for fuel terminal safety systems

**Storm Risk:** Every prioritization choice saves one parish at another's expense.

### 4. Spillway Decisions
If reservoir gets too full (storm surge + rainfall), spillway must open.
- Controlled release = flooding downstream (Sterling)
- No release = dam stress, potential failure
- Decision must be made hours before peak rain

**Storm Risk:** Heartland may have to flood Sterling to save the dam.

---

## Class Character

**Who lives here:**
- Dam operators (engineers, technicians)
- Water treatment specialists
- High-voltage electricians
- Heavy equipment operators
- Maintenance crews

**What they believe:**
- "We keep everything running. Nobody notices until we don't."
- "Physics doesn't negotiate. The dam does what the dam does."
- "Every parish thinks they're the priority. We see the whole grid."
- "When it goes bad, we're the ones who stay."

**What they fear:**
- Dam failure (they know it would kill people)
- Being blamed for prioritization choices
- Being cut off during the storm with no support
- Making the wrong call on spillway

**What they want:**
- Clear protocols (who's priority 1, 2, 3)
- Authority to match responsibility
- Recognition that they're making life-death decisions
- Backup—which they know they won't get in time

**Class tension with other parishes:**
- Summit: "They tell us to prioritize Keystone, then themselves, then tell us to 'figure it out' for everyone else."
- Keystone: "They need our power but don't think about what it takes to provide it."
- Sterling: "They're downstream. They don't understand we might have to flood them."
- Haven/Gateway: "They're far away. They don't think about us at all."

---
