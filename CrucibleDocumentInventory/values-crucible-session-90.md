# The 90-minute session

Ninety minutes: fifteen to brief, sixty to play in four quarters of fifteen, fifteen to debrief. Six tables, one parish each. One facilitator, who is also the DM and whose only required move during play is "next". The engine runs in live mode and the board is on the room's screen.

This replaces the 60-minute shape in [[decision-2026-10-19-nursing-leaders-60min]]. The conference, the twelve-card deck and the one-dilemma-per-table-per-quarter rule from that record stand.

## What the room looks like

- Six tables of five to eight, each with a parish tent card: Gateway, Heartland, Keystone, Haven, Summit, Sterling. Thirty to forty-eight people.
- The board on the main screen: the island, the routes, the two counters (doses shipped, people safe) and six card racks. [[concordia-storm-quarters]] gives it the storm; [[crucible-residents-v3]] gives it the faces.
- One facilitator at the DM laptop. A second person as board operator and runner helps; one person can do it alone if each table has a recorder entering choices on a tablet.
- Each table's pack: a one-page parish sheet (Produces, Needs, Uniquely holds, Shares, Tempted to hoard, from [[concordia-parishes-v3]]); the who-needs-whom table; three or four resident cards; the sealed secret brief; the token sheet; a twelve-card values deck; GRANT, REFUSE and PART cards; option cards A to D.

## Brief: 0:00 to 0:15

| Clock | Move | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | The frame. Concordia, Midas, the cure, the two counters. "The gap is the lesson": what you say you value at minute eight against what you do at minute fifty. | Facilitator |
| 0:03–0:08 | Values lock. Each person takes the twelve-card deck and chooses three in silence; the table then agrees on three in four minutes and writes them on the tent. Entered as the table's stated values. Locked at 0:08; nothing moves them afterwards. | Tables |
| 0:08–0:12 | Parish packs read at the table. The facilitator introduces the six personas in one line each and names the chain: kelp to concentrate to Celarithin to the ship. | Facilitator, tables |
| 0:12–0:15 | Secret briefs opened and read in silence. The three rules: only what is entered counts; a send is final; nobody sees your page until the debrief. "When I say next, the storm moves." | Facilitator |

Three values from twelve, not a ranking of forty, because the debrief has six minutes for the gap and three chips against three words is readable from the back of the room. [[values-crucible-card-deck]] has the full forty for longer formats.

## Play: 0:15 to 1:15

Each quarter is fifteen minutes with the same rhythm. The facilitator says "next"; the engine advances the storm to the quarter's window, resolves last quarter's entries, and the board changes. The facilitator reads what changed.

| Minutes | Move |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Narration from the quarter card in [[concordia-storm-quarters]]: weather, routes cut or reopened, the counters, resident card states read by name. |
| 2–8 | The dilemma. Six cards go up, one per table, from [[crucible-dilemmas-v3]]. The facilitator reads each persona's line in turn (ninety seconds for six), then the tables deliberate. The recorder enters A, B, C or D by minute 8. No entry is a choice too: the engine takes the option the persona would have taken alone, and the board says so. |
| 8–12 | The quarter's second move: allocation in preparation, calls for aid in approach and landfall, the repair round in recovery. |
| 12–15 | Consequences. The engine resolves choices and deliveries against the route table; the board shows the counters, the map and the card racks; the facilitator reads the changes. Thirty seconds of silence before "next". |

### Quarter 1, Preparation (0:15–0:30; T-72h to T-36h; intensity 3–4)

The second move is the allocation round ([[crucible-resource-tokens-v3]]). Because the round needs negotiation across tables, the dilemma and the sheet share one window: at minute 2 both go up, tables talk to each other across the room, and the sheet and the option are both due at minute 10. Minutes 10–12 are entry, 12–15 consequences. Sends made in this quarter always arrive.

### Quarter 2, Approach (0:30–0:45; T-36h to T-6h; intensity 5–7)

Narration carries the route-cut table: the harbour and the last flight at T-30h, the runway at T-24h, the Switchbacks at T-18h, the Gorge Road and the cell network at T-12h. The second move is calls for aid A1 to A7 ([[crucible-calls-for-aid-v3]]): at minute 8 the board shows each asked table its ask; the asked table holds up GRANT, REFUSE or PART within two minutes; the engine resolves delivery against what is still open; the asking table is told what arrived, not what was promised.

### Quarter 3, Landfall (0:45–1:00; T-6h to T+6h; intensity 8–10)

Narration: grid down by T-2h, the reservoir at 97%, the hospital's eleven minutes, surge on Southfield and the terminal, the eye at T+0. The dilemmas are the hardest in the deck and the persona lines are read slowly. Calls L1 to L8 follow; several can only be answered in the eye. Consequences are read by name from the card racks: safe, at risk, rescued, injured, lost.

### Quarter 4, Recovery (1:00–1:15; T+6h to T+48h; intensity 6 falling to 3)

Narration: routes reopen, the dam at 60%, the counters as they stand. The dilemmas turn to repair, giving back and credit. The second move is the **repair round**: any table may send any token it still holds to another parish, and every table writes one line of credit naming another table's people (a crew, a captain, a nurse) to be read at the debrief. The engine records sends as `service` and `stewardship` signals and credit lines as `recognition` given rather than taken. Consequences show the final counters.

## Debrief: 1:15 to 1:30

| Clock | Move |
|---|---|
| 1:15–1:17 | The two counters, read without comment: doses shipped (120,000, 96,000 or 0 of the Singapore consignment) and people safe of 24,100. Then the card racks: every name, every state. |
| 1:17–1:23 | The gap. Per table the board shows the three locked values beside the enacted chips from every source: dilemma options, allocation entries, aid answers, the repair round. The widest gap is highlighted. Three minutes at the table on one question: *where did we act against what we wrote at 0:08, and what was the pressure?* Then one sentence from each table to the room. |
| 1:23–1:27 | The secrets, read aloud in order: Sterling, Summit, Haven, Keystone, Heartland, Gateway ([[crucible-secret-briefs-v3]]). One question to the room: *which secret, on the table at T-72h, would have changed the most decisions?* |
| 1:27–1:30 | Credit lines read by the tables that wrote them. Each table writes one commitment: *next time the pressure is ___, we will ___.* One person per table reads it. Close. |

The debrief ends on what people did well and what they will do next, in that order. The room leaves clearer, not assessed ([[values-crucible-experience-arc]]).

## The facilitator's deck

Six pages: brief, four quarters, debrief. Each play page has the narration (eight lines), the six persona lines, the asks in that quarter and who answers them, and the two or three things the board will show that should be read by name. The facilitator never adjudicates: delivery, flood, rescue and the counters are the engine's, and the facilitator reads them. If a table argues with the board, the answer is "that is what happened; the debrief is where we talk about why".

## Engine mapping

- **Session profile.** A `nursing-90` profile beside `crucible/content/sessions/nursing-60.json`: the same twelve-card deck; `beats` for the four quarters as mapped in [[concordia-storm-quarters]]; `ask` lists of one `v3-*` id per table per quarter.
- **Live mode.** "Next" advances the engine to the quarter's window. The quarter clock is the facilitator's; the core keeps no wall clock.
- **Stated values.** Three ids per table from the deck, entered before the first "next".
- **New record types** the engine does not yet have: allocation entries (token, keep or send, destination), aid answers (ask id, GRANT, REFUSE or PART, quantity), repair-round sends and credit lines, resident card states. Each carries value signals into the gap computation beside dilemma options. This is engine work after Jerry's review (decision record, point 9); the schema change is itself a decision to record.
- **Determinism.** Every entry is an input in the run log; `crucible replay` must reproduce the board at every "next".

## What gives when time is short

Fifteen minutes a quarter is tight with six simultaneous dilemmas. What gives, in order: the thirty seconds of silence; the persona lines read aloud (print them on the cards and let the tables read); the repair round's token sends (keep the credit lines). What never gives: the values lock, the allocation round, the landfall consequences read by name, and the six minutes for the gap.
