# Secret briefs

Each table receives one private page in a sealed envelope at the brief. Nobody else sees it until the debrief, when all six are read aloud. Each secret is one of the three kinds in the decision record: a hidden stockpile, a private pressure from headquarters, or a fact about another parish. Several are two of those at once.

Design rule: each secret feeds at least one of the table's own dilemmas and tempts one hoard or one lie, so the debrief can ask: *you knew; what did you do with it?* The DM has a copy of all six and never refers to them during play.

## Gateway: the blocked seats

> **Private to the Gateway council.** The last departure at T-30h has 45 seats. Twelve of them were blocked yesterday by Sterling's corporate travel office for "Meridian personnel, names to follow". The airline has honoured the block because Meridian guarantees its winter charters. Summit does not know. The airport manager has told you because she wants someone above her to own it.
>
> You can release the block (the airline will comply if the parish asks in writing), keep it and say nothing, or tell Summit and let the Council fight Sterling.

*Kind:* a fact about another parish. *Feeds:* approach-gateway (the last plane). *Tempts:* using the twelve seats as currency with Sterling for the helicopters.

## Heartland: the gate and the tank

> **Private to the Authority board.** Spillway gate 2's actuator has a known fault. A controlled release takes ninety minutes longer to establish than the published protocol assumes, so the protocol's 97% trigger has to be treated as 94% to be safe. The board chose in June not to publish this until the repair budget was approved. Only Tanaka, the gate crew and you know.
>
> Also: the works manager reports four days of concentrate in the tanks. It is six. He pads the number so Keystone does not plan against it.

*Kind:* a private institutional pressure and a hidden stockpile. *Feeds:* approach-heartland (warn Riverside), landfall-heartland (94, 97 or 99), prep-heartland (whether to honour the Priority Schedule when you know the works can carry Keystone longer than Keystone thinks). *Tempts:* waiting for 97% and calling it protocol; keeping the two extra days.

## Keystone: the memo and the message

> **Private to the Keystone site leadership.** Two documents.
>
> 1. An internal engineering memo, June 2024: "Southfield storm drainage has not been load-tested since construction; recommend test before next season." Closed by Sterling facilities: "No action; budget cycle." The 2,400 people in Southfield have been told the housing is built to code. It is. It has never been tested.
>
> 2. An email from Chicago this morning, to you and to Webb: "The Singapore consignment must sail. Site leaders will be assessed this quarter on doses shipped. The bonus pool for Concordia is contingent on the reliability clause not being triggered. Keep the line running until the port closes."

*Kind:* a private pressure from headquarters and a fact about your own people that another parish buried. *Feeds:* prep-keystone (what we tell the shift), approach-keystone (batch 47), landfall-keystone (Southfield). *Tempts:* the kind silence; running the line one more shift.

## Haven: the real reserve and the master's word

> **Private to the Port Authority board.** The fuel terminal reports 500,000 gallons to the island's emergency inventory. The true figure is 640,000. The Authority has under-reported for six years to keep a reserve the island does not plan against, after the 2019 shortage when Summit requisitioned the terminal to the last drum. Nobody outside the Authority and the terminal supervisors knows.
>
> Also: Captain Ingrid Solheim, master of the *Kestrel Reach*, has told the harbourmaster privately that she sails at T-30h with whatever is aboard. She will not wait for batch 47 whatever Keystone or Sterling says, and she will not tell them that until she casts off.

*Kind:* a hidden stockpile and a fact about another parish's hope. *Feeds:* approach-haven (four tankers), approach-keystone through the hold-the-ship call, landfall-haven (what is really in Tank 3's neighbours). *Tempts:* sharing "fairly" on the false number; letting Keystone exhaust a crew for a ship that will not wait.

## Summit: the Governor's wish and the flood map

> **Private to the Island Emergency Council.** The Governor is at a conference on the mainland and cannot return before the runway closes. Her chief of staff called at T-70h: "No mandatory order until the track is certain. A mandatory order triggers the mainland's emergency declaration, the insurers' storm clauses, and a political fight with Meridian we do not need in an election year. Voluntary language until 85%."
>
> Also: the Council's hydrologist has the dam's release map. The spillway floods Riverside *and* Sterling's lower campus. Sterling's own insurance flood map, which it filed last year, shows the same thing, and Sterling quietly moved its data centre to the fourth floor in the spring. It has not moved the ground-floor labs or warned the Riverside households that rent from it.

*Kind:* a private pressure from headquarters and a fact about another parish. *Feeds:* prep-summit (the order), approach-heartland and landfall-heartland through the warning call, recovery-summit (the $40M and the statement). *Tempts:* the hedged order; using the flood map as leverage with Sterling rather than as a warning.

## Sterling: the garages and the model

> **Private to the Meridian crisis room.** Three things Chicago expects you to know and the island does not.
>
> 1. There are twenty small generators in executive garages on the headland, 96 hours of fuel under the tower and 21 days of food in the operations centre. None of it is on the island's emergency inventory. Facilities calls it "campus continuity".
> 2. Meridian's internal track model has put the direct-hit probability at 70% since T-72h, fifteen points above the public forecast. Chicago's instruction: "Confidential until the Singapore consignment sails. A public number from us moves the market and moves the port's crews."
> 3. The Whitfield twins, the Haddads and ten other executive households are on a helicopter manifest drawn up by Chicago's security team last night. Summit has not been told the helicopters exist as a resource.

*Kind:* a hidden stockpile and a private pressure from headquarters. *Feeds:* prep-sterling (the model), approach-sterling (two helicopters), landfall-sterling (the bunker door), the generator tokens. *Tempts:* everything.

## At the debrief

The DM reads all six aloud, in this order: Sterling, Summit, Haven, Keystone, Heartland, Gateway. Then one question to the room: *which secret, had it been on the table at T-72h, would have changed the most decisions?* The board shows, per table, the decisions its secret touched and what the table did at each.
