Values Crucible · phase 0 · first cut 2026-10-22

Paper prototype: Keystone and Haven

A printable kit for one hour with four to eight players and you as the Crucible master (CM). Two parishes, two teams each, twelve storm turns. It tests the rules decided on 2026-10-22 (storm turns, yields, two meters, promises, leaders, goals and projects) before any Godot code. Print it from your browser on letter paper; each section starts a new page.

Every number here is a first cut for the playtest to break. Names marked “placeholder” and all card wording are yours to change.

1 · CM guide: running the hour

What you need

  • This kit printed once. Cut out the tokens (section 8), the cards (6 and 7) and the slips (9).
  • About 200 poker chips or coins for coins.
  • One six-sided die: the storm die. It stands in for the game's seeded random roll.
  • A phone timer, a pen per team, and a sheet of paper for your CM log (section 10).

Players

Four teams, one to two people each:

  • Keystone community (KC): Southfield families and the skeleton crew.
  • Keystone Meridian (KM): the plant, the vault, the trucks.
  • Haven community (HC): Local 847 and the waterfront families.
  • Haven Meridian (HM): the berth, the cranes, the fuel terminal, the tug.

With three players, one person plays both Haven teams. With two, drop Haven and play Keystone alone with the off-board calls only.

The hour

MinWhat happens
0–5Read the opening (below). Hand out team sheets, tokens and the two secret cards (KM and HM only, face down).
5–10Stance. Each person picks three values on a stance card and folds it. Each team writes one sentence: “In this storm, we will…”
10–55Twelve storm turns in four quarters, with a 2-minute Mission brief after turns 3, 6 and 9 (standings, goals scored, one question).
55–65Debrief (section 11).

One storm turn (about 3 minutes)

  1. Read the storm hour and its news line from the run sheet. Read any moment or leader card that fires.
  2. Plan (2 minutes on the timer). All four teams at once: talk, move their own tokens, trade, write promises, vote on a moment. The owner of a decision announces the final call.
  3. “Hands off.” Then you resolve the turn in the order printed on the run sheet, and read one news line or social post.
Your stance as CM: you shape the questions and voice the leaders, but you never override a consequence. The engine is a witness, not a judge: say what happened, never which value it showed.

Opening (read aloud)

“Hurricane Midas is seventy-two hours out. Keystone makes Celarithin, the kelp cancer cure, for 340,000 patients. Haven ships it. The Kestrel Reach is at the berth, and she sails at the end of turn 4 with whatever is aboard. Each parish has two teams: the people who live here, and Meridian, the company that employs them. You will each be judged on one thing: the resources you still hold when the storm has passed. Most resources wins.”

2 · Player rules

How you win

Every team starts with 30. At the end, count: coins, plus 3 for each of your groups that is safe, plus 1 per dose or fuel token, plus the printed value of each asset still intact. Most resources wins.

Each turn

Two minutes to plan, all teams at once. Move your own tokens, trade, promise, vote. When the CM says “hands off”, the storm hour plays out.

Moving

  • A group walks one zone per turn inside its parish.
  • The roads to Summit and the coast road need a vehicle. A vehicle carries 1 group or 2 tokens (doses or fuel) one road per turn.
  • A family group may enter the Plant only as a worker or, if it is open, into the cafeteria shelter.
  • Roads close at the turn printed on the map. A closed road cannot be used.

Where a group can be

  • Home: earns 1 coin a turn. Not safe if its zone floods.
  • At a job (clean rooms, cranes): earns 1 coin for its team, and the job produces for Meridian.
  • Sheltered (union hall, cafeteria, Summit): safe, earns nothing.
  • Stranded: in a flooded zone. Earns nothing. Only a vehicle can bring it out, and only in the eye (t8) unless a card says otherwise: a truck into Southfield, the tug onto the Waterfront or the Berth. Rescued groups go to the Plant cafeteria (it opens for rescues) or the union hall. Still stranded at the end of turn 9, it is lost: remove it and your team pays 4 coins.

What Meridian’s assets do

  • Clean rooms (Keystone): +1 dose token a turn when a crew group works there and the plant has power.
  • Power: the grid fails at the end of turn 6. From turn 7 the plant’s generator burns 1 fuel a turn. With no fuel, the clean rooms stop and the vault spoils 2 doses a turn.
  • Cranes (Haven): +1 coin to Haven Meridian per crew group on them while the harbour is open. Each crew group loads 1 dose token aboard a turn. From turn 10 the cranes need 1 fuel a turn.
  • The ships: the Kestrel Reach sails at the end of turn 4. Each dose aboard pays Keystone Meridian 3 and Haven Meridian 1. The recovery ship sails at the end of turn 12 and pays 2 and 1.

The two meters (one pair per parish)

  • Community trust moves with how residents are treated. At 3 or below, crews walk off every Meridian job until it is back to 4.
  • Board confidence moves with how Meridian’s assets fare. At 3 or below, Chicago takes 2 coins a turn from the Meridian team until it is back to 4.

Between teams

  • Send anything as a gift, a loan (it comes back at the end if it survived) or a trade. Write it on a trade slip.
  • Promises count only on a promise slip signed by both teams. The CM checks it when it falls due; kept or broken, the news hears about it.

Decisions

When a moment card comes to your parish, both teams vote. The card names who makes the final call. Silence when the timer ends means the storm decided: the CM reads the card’s last line.

Resolve every turn in this order

  1. Moment and leader effects the teams chose.
  2. Moves that were made (check closed roads and vehicle loads).
  3. The storm: closures and floods from the table above. Mark stranded groups.
  4. Yields: each team takes its coins and dose tokens. Loading: crews put doses aboard.
  5. Upkeep: generator and crane fuel; vault spoilage; Chicago’s 2 coins if confidence is 3 or below.
  6. Meters (standard moves below), then promises that fall due.
  7. One news line or social post (section 10).

Standard meter moves

  • Trust +1: a group reaches shelter before its zone floods (once per group).
  • Trust −1: each stranded group, each turn. Trust −2: a group lost.
  • Trust ±1 in the receiving parish: a promise kept or broken.
  • Confidence +1: the Kestrel Reach sails with 6 or more doses. −1: fewer than 4.
  • Confidence −1: each turn the clean rooms are dark from t7.
  • Confidence −2: an asset destroyed (a truck lost, the cranes flooded, a spill).

When a meter reaches 3 or below, read that parish’s leader pressure card (section 7).

6 · Moment cards

Each card names the two values at stake and nothing more. Both teams of the parish vote; the owner makes the final call. If the timer runs out, read the card’s last line.

t1 · Haven · owner: Haven Meridian

Load now, or ask first

At stake: respect · stewardship

Loading takes most of four turns and must start now to fill the ship. Maria Santos wants a two-hour meeting on shift first: the real forecast, hazard pay, and who stays.

Maria: “You can have every crane I’ve got. Ask me first.”

  1. ALoad now; meet later. Haven crews are Tired (a Tired crew cannot take a peak job in t7–t8). Confidence +1, trust −1.
  2. BHold the meeting, share the forecast, pay hazard rate: Haven Meridian pays Haven community 2 coins. No loading this turn. Trust +1.
  3. CMeet, and let the union decide who stays: from now Haven community alone decides whether its crews are on the cranes. No loading this turn. Trust +2, confidence −1.

No answer: the crews start on their own (A without the confidence).

t2 · Keystone · owner: Keystone Meridian · hand over face down

The drainage memo

At stake: candor · compassion

The day shift wants to go home and prepare Southfield. You hold a 2024 memo: the flat’s storm drainage was never load-tested. Chicago says the line runs until the port closes. If you tell them, the crew walks and families drive to Summit in the rain with nowhere confirmed.

  1. ATell the shift and Southfield now. Show Keystone community your secret card. The crew group leaves the clean rooms for t2 and t3 (no production; no crew wage). Trust +2, confidence −1.
  2. BTell them when the line closes. The CM reveals the memo at the start of t5. Trust −1 at the reveal.
  3. CThe company stands by “built to code”. No reveal. Confidence +1. If any group is in Southfield when it floods: trust −3 and the news finds the memo.

No answer: C.

11 · Debrief

Mission briefs (2 minutes after t3, t6, t9)

  1. Read the standings and the goals met.
  2. Ask one question: “What did your team decide this quarter that you would not have decided an hour ago?”
  3. Announce the next quarter’s goals.

Final debrief

  1. Standings. Who won on resources. Then: “What did winning cost, and who paid it?”
  2. The secrets. Read both secret cards. “Which secret, on the table at turn 1, would have changed the most?”
  3. Stance against record. Each team reads its stance sentence. Read its rows of the CM log: the choice and the two values at stake. Ask: “Where did the choice match the stance? Where did it not, and what was pressing on you right then?”
  4. The two teams. “When community and Meridian disagreed, who made the call, and how did the other team find out?”
  5. The promises. Read the kept and broken ones.
  6. Commitment. Each person writes on the back of their stance card: “Next time pressure comes, I will…” and keeps it.
Witness, not judge: the CM says what happened and which values were at stake. The players say what it meant.

Pressure (from the decided rules)

  • A choice made after a leader pressure card is noted as made under pressure; giving way to it is what the dissertation scale calls capitulating.
  • Only off-board voices press: the leaders, Webb, Chicago, Sterling. Never another table.
  • Silence when the timer ends is no value signal: “the storm decided”. Ask about it.

The best score for a community team needs Meridian’s help: trucks, the cafeteria, or the truth about Southfield. That is the conflict the prototype is testing.

Weakest spot: Haven Meridian earns mostly from what other teams do. If doing nothing comes within 5 points of active play in your test, raise the flood cost for the cranes or make the recovery ship need Haven fuel.

Variant if the room is sharp

Haven Meridian’s secret card gets a second line: Captain Solheim will sail at the end of t3, not t4, whatever anyone says. Keystone loses a turn of loading unless Haven tells it.

What to watch and note

  1. Was 2 minutes enough to plan? (The Godot target is 60–90 seconds.)
  2. Did the two teams in a parish argue, or just cooperate? Where?
  3. Did anyone hoard, and did hoarding win?
  4. Did players read a moment card as a quiz with a right answer?
  5. Did the two meters change any decision?
  6. Were promises made? Kept?
  7. Did the leaders’ voices land as pressure, or as noise?
  8. Turns 7 and 8: too much at once for the CM?
  9. Which rule did you have to explain twice?
  10. How long did each quarter take?

Bring the notes back to the thread; the numbers that survive become the first content JSON for the Godot build and the bot balance gate.

What this prototype leaves out

The other three parishes, the hex grid, phones, generated news, the run log and replay, and the reflexive issue engine. Calls for aid from Summit and Heartland are read by the CM.
