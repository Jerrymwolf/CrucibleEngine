## d. Mechanics in our own words

Notation: U(a, b) is a uniform integer draw; "1 in 2^n" means a mask test as above. Numbers are the reference engine's tunings; section e proposes ours.

### d.1 Power connectivity scan

The reference world is a grid. Each tile carries a "conducts" flag: wires, zones, plants and stations conduct; ordinary road and rail tiles do not (only the special road-over-wire and rail-over-wire tiles do). Each generating plant is a trace origin and has a capacity measured not in watts but in *how many tiles it may energise*.

The scan, described rather than transcribed: clear the 1-bit grid. Sum the capacity of all plants counted in the last census into one global budget. Take trace origins off a stack one at a time and walk from each: mark the current tile powered and charge one unit against the budget; if the budget is exhausted, emit "insufficient power" and abandon the whole scan (everything not yet reached stays dark). Look at the four cardinal neighbours and count those that conduct and are not yet marked, stopping the count at two. If two or more, remember the current tile on the stack so the other branch is walked later. If at least one, step to the last such neighbour found and continue the walk; if none, the walk ends and the next stacked position is taken.

Consequences worth copying: (1) connectivity is recomputed from scratch each scan, so a cut wire disconnects everything downstream in one step; (2) capacity is global, not per-plant, and the fill is greedy and order-dependent, so an over-subscribed grid blacks out whichever region is traced last rather than everyone proportionally; (3) the source itself notes that an adjacent second plant is traced as a consumer, a known bug. Unpowered zones do not vanish: their growth score is replaced by a fixed negative value so they stall and slowly decline, unpowered stations work at half effect, and the city score is multiplied by the powered fraction. A warning message is raised when powered share drops below 70 percent.

### d.2 Disaster propagation

Trigger. Once per 16-phase cycle a single lottery runs with odds 1 in 480 / 240 / 60 by difficulty. If it hits, one of nine equally likely outcomes is chosen; two are fire, two flood, one tornado, one a no-op earthquake placeholder, one nothing, and two spawn a monster only when the pollution average exceeds 60. There is no "storm" object: weather is implicit in the lottery.

Fire. A burning cell is revisited every scan but acts on only a quarter of visits. On an acting visit it first attempts to spread: for each of the four neighbours independently, with probability 1/8, if that neighbour is combustible it ignites (a zone is destroyed outright and heavy industry additionally explodes). Then, on the same acting visit, the cell burns out with probability 1/(r+1), where r = 10 with no fire coverage, 3 for coverage in (0, 20], 2 for (20, 100], 1 above 100. Per scan that gives a burnout probability of 1/(4(r+1)), so expected lifetime goes from 44 scans (no coverage) through 16 and 12 to 8 (strong coverage). Expected ignition attempts per scan per burning cell are 4 x 1/4 x 1/8 = 1/8 regardless of coverage; coverage shortens the fire's life rather than its reach per scan. Fire coverage is a smoothed field produced by stations (see d.6). A zone catching fire also loses 20 points of local rate-of-growth memory, which decays back toward zero at 1 point per 5 cycles.

Flood. Ignition picks a water cell with a floodable land neighbour and arms a countdown of 30 cycles. While armed, each flood cell tries each neighbour with probability 1/8 per scan; burnable, bare or wooded land floods (zones destroyed). After the countdown expires, each flood cell recedes with probability 1/16 per scan, i.e. expected 16 scans to dry. So a flood has an expanding phase of fixed duration and a geometric recession tail.

Radiation: a meltdown sets the 4 by 4 plant footprint on fire and takes 200 random samples in a 41 by 31 box around it, turning combustible or bare non-zone cells radioactive; each decays with probability 1/4096 per scan (a very long tail by design). Earthquake (user-triggered only at this commit): 300 to 1000 random cells; of those that are vulnerable, 3 in 4 become rubble, 1 in 4 fire.

### d.3 Feedback loops

Demand. Three accumulators (residential, commercial, industrial "valves") hold the pressure to grow or shrink. Every second cycle the update is, in our notation:

```
residents := res_population / 8
jobs := commercial + industrial population from the previous census sample
employment := jobs / residents (1 if no residents)
projected_residents := residents * (1 + (employment - 1) + 0.02)      // migration + births
labour := clamp(residents_sample / jobs_sample, 0, 1.3)               // 1 if no jobs
projected_commercial := (residents + commercial + industrial) / 3.7 * labour
projected_industrial := max(industrial * labour * external_factor[difficulty], 5)   // 1.2, 1.1, 0.98
ratio_x := min(projected_x / current_x, 2)   // per sector; 1.3 for residential and projected_x for the others when current_x is 0
delta_x := (ratio_x - 1) * 600 + tax_penalty(min(tax_rate + difficulty, 20))
valve_x := clamp(valve_x + round(delta_x), -range_x, +range_x)          // 2000, 1500, 1500
if sector is capped (missing prerequisite building) and valve_x > 0: valve_x := 0
```

The tax penalty is a monotone table from +200 at zero tax through zero at 7 percent to -600 at 20 percent. Because deltas are added to an accumulator with a range, the valves are an integrator with saturation: demand lags conditions and persists after they change. That is the main source of inertia.

Migration per zone. Each zone, when scanned, adds the valve to a location score (land value minus pollution, mapped to -3000..3000; forced to -3000 with no road; the whole sum replaced by -500 when unpowered) and compares against a signed 16-bit draw offset by a large bias so that roughly 9 percent of assessments are unconditional growth, roughly 10 percent unconditional decline, and only a thin band depends on the score; a score below -350 can never grow and one above +350 can never decline. Assessment itself happens on 1 in 8 scans (always for empty zones). Net effect: growth and decline are slow stochastic ratchets whose bias is set by demand plus local amenity.

Land value, pollution, crime. Pollution is emitted by traffic, industry, coal plants, fire and radiation, then diffused by two passes of a 4-neighbour box blur. Land value = centrality term ((34 minus half the Manhattan distance to the zone centroid, distance capped at 64) times 4) + terrain + (minus) pollution, minus 20 if crime is extreme, clamped 1..250. Crime = (128 - land value) + population density, capped 300, minus smoothed police coverage, clamped 0..250. Loops: pollution lowers land value, low land value raises crime, high crime lowers land value, both lower the location score, which lowers growth, which lowers jobs, which lowers the residential valve.

Tax base. Income = floor(population * mean_land_value / 120) * tax_rate * difficulty factor. Tax base is therefore population times land value, so pollution and crime shrink revenue, and the tax rate feeds back negatively into demand through the penalty table. Spending is prioritised road, then fire, then police; each service's effectiveness is max effect times the funded fraction, so underfunding degrades coverage fields and road condition probabilistically (1 in 512 per road tile per scan once funding drops below 15/16).

Smoothing and inertia appear in five places: valve accumulators (integrator), rate-of-growth memory (decays 1 per 5 cycles), traffic density (decays 24 per cycle), census ramps for crime and pollution (quarter-step toward target every 4 time units, i.e. alpha 0.25), and the city score (mean of previous and new, alpha 0.5). Approval is a Monte Carlo of 100 voters against the score, and complaints are votes of up to 100 against a uniform 0..300 tolerance.

### d.4 Simulation cadence

The clock is a 16-phase cycle. Phase 0 advances city time by one unit, recomputes demand on even cycles and clears per-cycle census counters. Phases 1-8 each scan one eighth of the map's columns, running every tile's handler (zone growth, fire, flood, roads, services, plants). Phase 9 takes a census every 4 time units, a long census every 40, and collects tax and runs the evaluation every 48. Phase 10 decays the transient maps. Phases 11-15 run the global field updates (power, pollution/land value, crime, density, fire coverage) and the disaster lottery, each gated by a divisor that grows with speed so that at higher speed the expensive fields are refreshed less often per unit of city time (power every 2/4/5 cycles, pollution every 2/7/17, crime 1/8/18, density 1/9/19, fire 1/10/20). Speed otherwise only changes the minimum wall-clock gap between phases (100, 50, 10 ms). Repair of building footprints is tied to the city-time counter through a period mask (power plants: every 8 units), and only touches footprint tiles that have been cleared or overbuilt (d.6).

### d.5 Randomness discipline

The reference port draws from the host's unseeded generator through five helpers: uniform integer in 0..n inclusive, 16-bit, signed 16-bit, a mask "chance" test, and a minimum-of-two biased draw. Twenty-four modules call it; the order of calls follows the scan order, so even with a seed, output would depend on scan order and on the wall-clock-gated phase scheduler. The lessons are negative: there is no single RNG stream, no seeding, and no replay. What is worth borrowing is the *vocabulary* of draws (chance-with-mask gives cheap power-of-two probabilities; min-of-two gives a cheap skew toward low values) and the fact that every stochastic decision is local and single-draw, which makes a deterministic re-implementation straightforward.

### d.6 Emergency services and repair

Each station, when scanned, deposits an effect at its block equal to the budget-derived service effect (max 1000 at full funding), halved if the station is unpowered and halved again if it is not adjacent to a road. The deposit map is then blurred three times to form a coverage field with roughly a 3-block radius. Fire coverage changes the burnout odds (d.2); police coverage subtracts directly from crime (d.3). Repair is automatic but narrow: on the period timer, footprint tiles of a building that are bare ground, water, trees, or stray road/wire/building tiles are rewritten to the canonical building tile; tiles that are rubble, burning, flooded or radioactive are not. So a plant that burns does not come back until the rubble is bulldozed, at which point it is rebuilt for free on the next timer tick. Hospitals are meant to be spawned by strong residential demand on empty zones with 1-in-4 odds when the census reports a hospital need, and hospitals have no mechanical effect beyond assessed value; as shipped the need signal is never set (census.js:122-129 reads a misspelt field), so no hospital is ever built by the simulation.

## e. Adaptation to the Crucible Engine

Crucible's world (`crucible/content/concordia.json`) is six parishes: Heartland (dam, power and water source), Summit (comms primary), Gateway (airport, external comms), Haven (port), Keystone (biotech manufacture), Sterling (government, hospital). Links are the ring road (Gateway-Sterling-Haven-Keystone-Gateway, 42 km, `RING_ROAD`) and crater roads (`CRATER_ROADS`: ring to Summit and ring to Heartland, both `stormPassable: false`; Summit-Heartland, `floodRisk: true`), plus the dependency graph in `PARISH_DEPENDENCIES`. Ticks are 15 minutes from t = -4320 to t = 1440 (384 ticks; `ENGINE_SPEC.md`, "Model"). Systems are power, communications, roads, water, hospital, port, airport on a 0..100 scale with thresholds FAILED 20, CRITICAL 30, DEGRADED 50, OPERATIONAL 80 (`infrastructure.json`, `INFRASTRUCTURE_THRESHOLDS`). Timeline windows (`timeline.json`): watch-72, watch-48, warning-24, approach-12, approach-6, landfall-0, eye-6, recovery-24.

### e.1 Connectivity flood-fill with backup hours (spec "Model", step 3)

Mapping: power source = Heartland; conductive edges = the dependency edges plus crater roads for the physical line; the "capacity" idea becomes a dam output level. Proposal:

- Build the power graph from `INFRASTRUCTURE.power` (source Heartland, priority list Keystone then Summit) and `PARISH_DEPENDENCIES` edges where Heartland is upstream. Default edges: Heartland to Keystone, Haven, Gateway, Sterling and Summit (all five listed in Heartland's `providesTo`). Each edge is operational when both endpoints' `roads` are at or above FAILED (20) and the edge is not one of the two `stormPassable: false` crater segments during the landfall-0 and eye-6 windows; the Summit-Heartland edge (`floodRisk: true`) also fails while Heartland `water` is below CRITICAL (30).
- Each tick, breadth-first from Heartland if Heartland `power` is at or above 20; a reached parish is `grid_connected`. Keep the reference's single global capacity: capacity_units = floor(Heartland.power / 20) (0..5 parishes). Serve parishes in priority order Keystone, Summit, then by `population` descending (Sterling 6200, Haven 5400, Gateway 3800); parishes beyond capacity are treated as unreached. This reproduces the "last traced region blacks out" behaviour deterministically instead of by scan order.
- Backup. The content has two generator facts: `INFRASTRUCTURE.power.backupGenerators` (true for Summit, Keystone, Gateway, Heartland; false for Sterling, Haven) and a per-parish `resources.generators` count (Keystone 6, Haven 8, Gateway 15, Heartland 25, Summit 12, Sterling 10). Spec step 3 says backup "buys a limited number of hours" from `resources`, so: an unreached parish whose flag is true holds `power` at min(current, 60) for generator_hours = `resources.generators` hours, i.e. Keystone 24 ticks, Summit 48, Gateway 60, Heartland 100 (Heartland only when its own `power` is below 20), decremented only while disconnected. Haven and Sterling have generator counts but the flag is false; the content owner should resolve that inconsistency, and until then the flag wins. After fuel is exhausted, or for flag-false parishes immediately, power drops 10 per tick (`DEGRADATION_RATES.crisis.power` = 10; the reference instead pins unpowered zones to a fixed negative growth score, which has no analogue on a 0..100 level). Restoration raises power 5 per tick toward 100 (`DEGRADATION_RATES.recovery.power` is -5, i.e. 5 points of healing per tick).
- Mirror the reference's half-effect rule: a parish's `hospital` and `communications` effectiveness, as used by cascades and dilemma triggers, is halved while on generator and quartered when fully unpowered.
- Roads: a parish with no path to any other parish over edges whose `roads` on both ends are at or above 20 is `isolated` (spec step 3 already says this). Add the reference's "no road, no growth" rule as: an isolated parish's morale recovery rate (e.3) is zero.
- Spec change: step 3 says "loses that system at a bounded rate per tick unless it has local backup"; make the loss rate, the restore rate and the fuel-hours rule explicit constants in `infrastructure.json` under a new `CONNECTIVITY` block (`capacityLevelPerParish: 20`, `generatorHoldLevel: 60`, `lossPerTick: 10`, `restorePerTick: 5`), add the capacity rule, and specify restoration separately from loss. Reason: the reference shows that asymmetry (instant loss of connectivity, slow repair) is what makes outages feel consequential, and the proptest "levels stay within 0..100" needs the rates to be bounded.

### e.2 Probabilistic spread of damage across adjacent parishes (spec steps 2 and 4)

The reference's fire and flood are neighbour-wise Bernoulli trials with a fixed per-neighbour probability and a geometric burnout. On a six-node graph:

- Define `damage_front[parish][system]` as boolean "actively failing" when a system is at or below CRITICAL (30). Each tick, for each failing (parish, system) and each neighbour over the physical graph (ring road and crater roads for roads/power/water; dependency edges for port/airport/hospital), with probability p_spread the neighbour's same system loses spread_amount. `intensity.json` defines ten named levels and seven levers but no numeric multipliers, so the scaling must be a new content constant: propose p_spread = intensity / 40 per neighbour per tick, which is the reference's 1/8 at intensity 5, 1/16 at 2.5, and 1/4 at 10 (0.225 at the crisis ceiling of 9). spread_amount = 5 points, the `degradation` event threshold in spec step 2.
- Burnout: a failing system stops being a front with probability 1/(r+1) per tick where r = 10 if the parish's coverage proxy is below FAILED (20), 3 below DEGRADED (50), 2 below OPERATIONAL (80), 1 otherwise. This is deliberately four times the reference's effective per-scan rate (the reference only rolls on a quarter of scans; see d.2), because a 15-minute tick is coarser than a map scan. "Coverage" here = mean of the parish's own `roads` and `hospital` plus one quarter of each neighbour's (a one-pass blur, the graph analogue of the reference's three box blurs).
- Flood analogue: at the landfall-0 transition, arm a flood countdown of 24 ticks (6 h, the length of the landfall window before eye-6) for Heartland `water` and Haven `port`; re-arm for another 24 ticks at eye-6. While armed, spread as above with p_spread to neighbours over the `floodRisk` crater edge and the ring segments touching Haven; after expiry, each flooded system recovers with probability 1/16 per tick, at which point the normal `DEGRADATION_RATES.recovery` rate applies.
- Keep the existing `CASCADE_RULES` (16 rules) as the deterministic layer (they fire once at threshold, like the reference's meltdown spawning radiation), and make the probabilistic spread the stochastic layer. Spec change: step 4 should say cascades are deterministic and once-only, and a new step 4b "stochastic spread" sits between cascades and scheduled effects, with its constants in `infrastructure.json` (`SPREAD_RULES: {pPerNeighbourPerIntensity: 0.025, amount: 5, burnoutRates: [10, 3, 2, 1]}`).

### e.3 Feedback loops with inertia for morale and services (spec "Model", step 9 snapshot)

Replace the three demand valves with one integrator per parish: `morale_pressure` in -2000..2000, updated every 4 ticks (hourly) by delta = (service_index - 1) * 600 + leadership_term, where service_index = weighted mean of the parish's seven systems divided by OPERATIONAL (80) so that parity is at 80, and leadership_term is the mean of the parish's decision value signals over the last 4 ticks on the -3..3 scale times 50. Morale itself (0..100) moves each tick toward morale_target = 50 + morale_pressure / 40 (so the integrator's range maps onto 0..100) with alpha 0.25, the reference's census ramp. Expose a city-style score computed with the reference's shape: start from (250 - min(sum_of_deficits / 3, 250)) * 4, where the seven deficits are (100 - level) per system (sum at most 700, so sum/3 is at most 233 and the 250 clamp never binds; the reference sums seven 0..255 levels and divides by 3, where the clamp can bind); multiply by the connected fraction of parishes (the powered-fraction rule); apply at most 10 percent penalty per service with budget pressure (e.3 tax analogue) above zero; clamp 0..1000; and smooth with alpha 0.5 every 4 ticks. Approval for the facilitator view: 100 seeded voters comparing U(0, 1000) to the score. Spec change: "Model" has morale in the step 9 snapshot but no update rule; add this rule with the two alphas, the integrator range and the 40 divisor as content constants (`MORALE: {integratorRange: 2000, hourlyGain: 600, leadershipGain: 50, targetAlpha: 0.25, scoreAlpha: 0.5}` in `infrastructure.json` or a new `morale.json`).

Tax base analogue: `SUPPLY_CHAIN.interruptionCostPerHour` (2,400,000) times the blocked fraction of the 28-hour cycle (`SUPPLY_CHAIN.totalCycleHours`) becomes the budget pressure. A supply-chain step is blocked when the governing system of its parish is below DEGRADED (50): `airport` for the Gateway harvest step, `port` for the Haven transport and ship steps, `roads` for the Haven process step, `power` for the Keystone manufacture step. Budget pressure per tick = 2,400,000 x 0.25 h x (sum of blocked steps' `hoursToNext` / 28). Spending priority (reference: road, fire, police) becomes roads, hospital, communications in the recovery-24 window; each system's restore rate is scaled by its funded fraction, as the reference scales station effect by funded fraction. This gives the facilitator a lever equivalent to the reference tax slider.

### e.4 Cadence per 15-minute tick (spec "Model", steps 1-9)

Reference lesson: do cheap local updates every tick and expensive global fields on divisors; keep a fixed phase order. Proposal, replacing the nine-step list with the same steps but explicit divisors:

| Every tick | Every 2 ticks | Every 4 ticks (hourly) | Every 16 ticks (4 h) | Every 96 ticks (24 h) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| weather (step 1), degradation (2), connectivity fill (3), scheduled effects (5), snapshot (9) | stochastic spread (4b), cascade evaluation (4) | morale integrator (e.3), service coverage blur (e.2), dilemma selection check (6) | shock lottery for off-timeline events (below) | summary roll-ups |

Shock lottery: one draw every 16 ticks with odds by intensity 1 in 480 at intensity 1-2, 1 in 240 at 3-4, 1 in 120 at 5-6, 1 in 60 at 7-10 (the reference's three difficulty odds plus one interpolated step). On a hit, pick one of nine equally likely slots as the reference does: slots 1-2 a 15-point hit to `roads` of a random parish; slots 3-4 a 15-point hit to Heartland `water` (flood analogue); slot 5 a 15-point hit to Summit `communications` (tornado analogue); slots 6-7 nothing; slots 8-9 a 15-point hit to Haven `port`, only while storm surge in the current window is above zero (the reference's "monster only when pollution is high" gate). Intensity (the Crucible Master dial) plays the role of the reference's speed divisors: higher intensity raises spread probability and dilemma cadence but must never change the order of phases.
